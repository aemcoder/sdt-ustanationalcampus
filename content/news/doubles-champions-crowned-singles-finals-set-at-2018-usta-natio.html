Doubles Champions Crowned; Singles Finals Set

January 3, 2019

style
article-hero

The matchups for the singles finals were set and three of the four championship doubles tandems were crowned by the end of play Thursday at the 2018 USTA National Winter Championships, part of the USTA adidas Junior National Championship Series.

The boys’ and girls’ 18s singles finals will be broadcast live on Friday starting at 10 a.m. and can be viewed here.

In a rather unexpected turn of events, only three matches went the distance despite being in the late stages of the tournament.

Taking away Gold Balls in doubles action were No. 14 seeds Samir Banerjee (Basking Ridge, N.J.) and Louis Cloud i(San Antonio) in the boys’ 16s, top seeds Ava Catanzarite (Pittsburgh) and Avery Durham (Hilton Head Island, S.C.) in the girls’ 16s, and No. 15 Anna Campana (Hillsborough, Calif.) and Connie Ma (Dublin, Calif.) in the girls’ 18s.

For Banerjee and Cloud, they shook off the disappointment of failing to close out the final in two sets to turn in an almost flawless third in their win over No. 12 Jacob Bickersteth (West Bloomfield, Mich.) and Billy Suarez (Huntington, N.Y.) 6-2, 4-6, 6-1. Catanzarite and Durham closed out their reign as the top-rated tandem by besting the fourth-seeded duo of Carrie Beckman (Louisville, Ky.) and Emma Charney (Prospect, Ky.) 6-4, 6-3. Ma kept her hopes of taking both the singles and doubles titles alive thanks to her and Campana proving to be too tough for No. 4 Allie Gretkowski (Mount Pleasant, S.C.) and Anna Ross (Charleston, S.C.), earning the win 6-4, 6-2. The boys’ 18s doubles final will be contested on Friday, with the unseeded team of Hunter Heck (Saint Paul, Minn.) and JJ Tracy (Hilton Head Island, S.C.) facing No. 10 seeds Jeremie Casabon (Flourtown, Pa.) and Henry Ruger (Philadelphia).

The girls’ 16s championship tilt will feature No. 1 Leyden Games (Irvine, Calif.) looking to cap off her run to the title versus eighth-seeded Nadejda Maslova (Brooklyn, N.Y.). Neither was seriously threatened in the semifinals, as Games raced past No. 7 Bridget Stammel (Dallas) 6-3, 6-1 and Maslova handled DJ Bennett (Belleview, Fla.) 6-4, 6-1. It was the sixth time in six matches that Games won in straight sets and marked the fewest number of games she dropped in a contest since the opening round. Maslova has been even more commanding in reaching the final, being pushed to 6-4 in a set only twice while taking six sets by a 6-0 or 6-1 margin.

Playing for the crown in the girls’ 18s division will be local talent No. 3 Alana Wolfberg (Orlando) and No. 4 Connie Ma. In a similar story to the 16s, Wolfberg and Ma were clearly the two most dominant players over the course of the week. The home standing Wolfberg has been clutch on almost every crucial point she’s faced, which once again proved to be the case in her hard-fought 7-6(4), 6-4 victory against unseeded India Houghton (Belvedere Tiburon, Calif.). Meanwhile, Ma has seemingly gotten stronger with each match, easing her way into Friday with a 6-2, 6-1 effort against No. 8 Carly Briggs (Calhoun, Ga.).

Fifth-seeded Luke Casper (Santa Cruz, Calif.) backed up his upset over No. 1 Jack Anthrop in the quarterfinals in emphatic fashion, claiming the opening frame 6-1 before holding off No. 11 Samir Banerjee 7-5 in the second. That earned him a berth in the championship contest opposite No. 13 Quinn Snyder (Delran, N.J.), who has seemingly played with no pressure after ousting second seed Jacob Bickersteth 6-1, 1-6, 7-6(3) in the round of 16. Snyder took a one-sided affair with No. 17 John Kim Sunnyvale, Calif.) by a 6-3, 6-0 score line on Thursday.

The drama of the day took place in the boys’ 18s, where both semifinals could have gone either way. No. 9 Zachery Lim (Fairfield, Calif.) and No. 14 Daniel Milavsky (Needham, Mass.) battled in an opening set that lasted well over one hour. Lim surged ahead 4-2 with the first break of the match, but Milavsky broke right back on his sixth break point of the game. Lim would eventually take the frame in a tiebreak, seven points to four. Taking full advantage of having the momentum on his side, Lim broke to open the second then saved two break points to take full control. Milavsky was able to stay close until 3-2, when Lim won the final three games.

In the other semifinal, it appeared the match would belong to fourth seed Floridian Logan Zapp (Fleming Island, Fla.), but nothing would come easy for him or No. 17 Ishaan Ravichander (Belle Mead, N.J.). The first game featured 14 points in total and the opening three games all went to the returner. From there, Zapp and Ravichander stabilized themselves on serve, with Zapp closing things out 6-4. Ravichander survived being broken to start the second, responding by winning five of the next six games en route to grabbing the frame 6-3. He dug himself an even bigger hole in the decisive third, seeing Zapp sprint out to a 3-0 advantage. However, Ravichander staged a stunning rally, breaking in three consecutive times to fuel a spirited effort in which he won the last six games of the thrilling match.
