Six Straight for Southern

November 17, 2019

It was once again a two-team race at the 2019 USTA Keith Donoff Cup, the annual men’s 55 & Over Intersectional championship being hosted by the USTA National Campus for the second time in the history of the event. Ultimately, just like the previous five years, the men representing the Southern Section came away victorious, edging out the Florida Section by a mere two courts.

The Donoff Cup is composed of the following age divisions: 55s, 60s, 65s, and 70s. The Florida, Mid-Atlantic, Middles States, Midwest and Southern Sections each fielded a crew to compete those divisions, where they faced off in round robin dual matches. A dual match consisted of three singles and two doubles courts per division, each counting for one team point. 

Southern was nearly perfect in the 65s divisions, tallying 19 out of a possible 20 points in the division, while they also played their way to another 18 points in the 60s division. That effort proved to be the difference in the comparison with Florida, which came away with 13 and 15 points in those divisions, respectively. 

Florida countered by racking up 18 points in the 70s competition and tacked on another 17 in the 55s; meanwhile, Southern managed only 13 in the 70s and 15 in the 55s.

When it was all said and done, Southern boasted a total of 65 courts won, with Florida’s valiant effort coming up just shy in the form of 63 contests earned.

Below is a full roster of the men representing the champions of the 2019 Donoff Cup

55s Division: William C Bibb, Al Cannon, Brad Goodman, Davy Hairston, and Nik Makarenko

60s Division: Steven Garman, Ken Koelling, Drew Meyers, Mark Thompson, John Valastro

65s Division: Danny Carlson, Marvin Pearson, Keith Richardson, Tom Smith, and Doug Stursma

70s Division: John Chryst, Stephen Duffel, Richard U Howell, Richard Shipman, and Hugh Thomson

After not competing in 2018, the Mid-Atlantic group placed third overall with 33 points, 10 of which came from the men in the 70s division. Midwest finished fourth with 21 points, three better than fifth-place Middle States.
