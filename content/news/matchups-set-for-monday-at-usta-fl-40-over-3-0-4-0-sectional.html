Matchups Set for Monday

The second day of the 2018 USTA Florida 40 & Over 3.0/4.0 Sectional Championships, hosted by the USTA National Campus, was played from start to finish under mostly sunny skies Sunday. Action included the champion in the men’s 3.0 division being crowned along with the other divisions being whittled down to the semifinal and final stages.

The women's 3.0 and 4.0 semifinals are slated for 7:30 a.m. on Monday; the men's 4.0 final will get underway at 9:00 a.m.

The women’s 3.0 division featured one of the most comprehensive performances of the weekend, as Orange/Seminole (Star Island) posted a perfect 4-0 record in Flight 1 on the strength of 17 court victories while surrendering a mere seven sets. Duval (Charles J. Williams YMCA) also went undefeated in Flight 3, just edging out Broward (Midtown) to move into Monday’s semifinals. Hillsborough (Northdale) earned the nod in Flight 2 over Leon (Forestmeadows Park and Athletic Center) thanks to 11 individual matchup wins compared to just seven for Leon. However, the disappointment for Leon was short-lived, as they were awarded the wild card spot, where they will take on Duval. Orange/Seminole and Hillsborough will be opponents in the other semifinal.

In the 3.0 men’s competition, Leon (Forestmeadows Park and Athletic Center) ended with a 3-1 team record to prevail over Duval (Jax Golf) and Alachua (Jonesville Tennis Center). It was Duval who ended Sunday with more individual court wins and fewer sets lost than Leon, but Leon’s pair of head-to-head victories clinched the title for them. Alachua did defeat Leon once; however, that was their lone victory of the tournament.

For the women’s 4.0 division, two teams cruised in their flights to lock up a semifinal berth while two others needed the tiebreaking procedure to push them through. SMD (Coral Oaks) went 3-0 in Flight 3, posting an impressive 13 court victories while losing only nine sets. Hillsborough (Westchase) claimed Flight 4 in almost as dominant a fashion, winning 11 individual matchups. Collier (Heritage Bay) ended the day tied with Orange/Seminole (Sanlando) and Duval (Eagle Landing) at 2-1 overall, but earned the semifinal slot thanks to 11 individual court wins. Broward (Frank Veltri Tennis Center) survived via the same fate, as their nine courts won bested both Brevard (Rockledge Park) and Sarasota (Lakewood Ranch Athletic).

In Monday’s semifinal encounters, Broward is set to clash with Collier and SMD faces Hillsborough.

Three teams dominated throughout flight play in the men’s 4.0 division to move on to the semifinals. Orange/Seminole (Villagewalk Lake Nona) swept the field in Flight 1, taking 13 of the 15 individual courts in the process. SMD (Tamiami Park) put together an equally comprehensive effort in Flight 3, while Broward (Weston TC) won just one less court (12) en route to finishing atop Flight 2. Claiming the wild card spot was Duval (Charles J. Williams YMCA), the runners-up in Flight 2, on the strength of a 10-5 record in the 15 individual matchups they contested.

Orange/Seminole took down Broward by the final margin of 4-1 in one semifinal and SMD got past Duval by the same scoreline in the other. Orange/Seminole received a break when Broward’s top doubles duo retired at 4-1 in the first set. They grabbed full control of the dual thanks to Julio Infante and Gaurang Gandhi winning 6-2, 6-4 at line two. Orange/Seminole completed the doubles sweep behind Pedro Estrada and Luis Mir’s 7-6, 2-6, 1-0 triumph. In singles action, Hervin Alvarez outlasted Roberto Nieves 6-4, 4-6, 1-0 at the top spot for Orange/Seminole and Hugo Moreno earned the lone individual win for Broward in the second position.

SMD earned three courts in straight sets to clinch their berth in the championship dual. John Naus dominated 6-1, 6-2 at line one singles, while Jose Zambrano and Ricardo Deliz breezed through the top doubles spot 6-1, 6-3. Nelson Rodriguez and Jesus Minjares battled to a 7-5, 6-3 win at line three. Marion Hidalgo gave SMD their final victory in the second singles postion, 2-6, 6-2, 1-0. Duval salvaged the second doubles court, taking it 6-3, 6-3.

Established in 1980, USTA League has grown from 13,000 players in a few parts of the country in its first year to more than 900,000 players across the nation today, making it the world’s largest recreational tennis league.

USTA League was established to provide adult recreational players throughout the country with the opportunity to compete against players of similar ability levels. Players participate on teams in a league format, which is administered by the USTA through its 17 sections. The league groups players by using six National Tennis Rating Program (NTRP) levels, ranging from 2.5 (entry) to 5.0 (advanced). USTA League is open to any USTA member 18 years of age or older.
