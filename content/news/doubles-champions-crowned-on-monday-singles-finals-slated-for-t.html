Doubles Champions Crowned on Monday, Singles Finals Slated for Tuesday

January 2, 2018

Four doubles duos were crowned champions on Monday at the 2017 USTA National Winter Championships for the 16s and 18s age divisions, hosted by the USTA National Campus. Rain fell steadily for most of the day, forcing all consolation matches to be postpones while main draw play was moved indoors.

Earning USTA gold balls on day six of the prestigious event were Keshav Chopra and Coy Simon (boys’ 16s), Phillip Jordan and Andres Martin (boys’ 18s), Carmen Corley and Gianna Pielet (girls’ 16s), and Kolie Allen and Ivanna Corley (girls’ 18s).

No. 5 seeds Chopra (Marietta, Ga.) and Simon (Charleston, S.C.) were up against unseeded Matthew Mu (La Jolla, Calif.) and Scott Sculley (Hilton Head Island, S.C.) in the boys’ 16s final. The upset-minded Mu and Sculley won the opening set 6-2 but Chopra and Simon responded by taking the second by the same margin. The final frame was decided by mere points, but in the end it was Chopra and Simon emerging victorious in a decisive tiebreak, seven points to five.  

Jordan (Spartanburg, S.C.) and Martin (Flowery Branch, Ga.), seeded 11th, had been tested throughout their run to the boys’ 18s finals, knocking out the first seeds in the round of 16 as well as surviving a marathon semifinal battle. Their match with unseeded Alexander Kotzen (Short Hills, N.J.) and Patrick Maloney (Oyster Bay, N.Y.) was no different. Jordan and Martin took a big step toward winning the title by grabbing the opening set 7-6(5), and could have closed it out in straight sets. However, Kotzen and Maloney stayed alive with a 7-5 win in set two. The third was much more one-sided, ending 6-2 in favor of the 11th-seeded team.

One win away from defending their top seed billing, Carmen Corley (Albuquerque, N.M.) and Pielet (El Paso, Texas) turned in their most comprehensive performance of the event. Going head-to-head with 15th seeds Justine Dondonay (Alhambra, Calif.) and Anessa Lee (San Marino, Calif.), the No. 1 seeds cruised through the first set 6-2 and held off their opponents in a tense second frame, 6-4. The total of six games lost was the fewest of any match Corley and Pielet won en route to the championship.

Not to be outdone by her younger sister, Ivanna Corley (Albuquerque, N.M.) teamed up with Allen to steamroll through the girls’ 18s draw. Also the top seed in the division, Corley and Allen went up against Moorestown, N.J., sister Clarissa and Christina Hand. The top-seeded pair put the finishing touches on an incredible week of play, winning the match 6-1, 6-4. Corley and Allen did not surrender one set in five matches, giving away just 22 games in total.

Both of the 16s finals will feature a player looking to collect a second gold ball for the tournament. On the boy’s side, eighth seed Chopra ran his streak to 12 consecutive sets won after handling JJ Tracy (Hilton Head Island, S.C.) 6-4, 6-0. Chopra has only been pushed beyond 6-4 in two sets so far. His opponent in championship contest will be No. 6 seed Harsh Parikh (Tucson, Ariz.). The native of the Grand Canyon State fought past ninth-seeded Logan Zapp (Fleming Island, Fla.) 7-6(7), 7-5 on Monday.

Meanwhile Pielet was the beneficiary of a retirement in girls’ 16s semifinal action. She faced a very familiar opponent in Allura Zamarripa (Saint Helena, Calif.), having played her in doubles a day earlier. The opening set of their singles encounter was a lengthy one, eventually going in favor of the top seed 7-6(5). In the second, Pielet held a break advantage at 4-2 when Zamarripa retired due to injury.  The final obstacle standing between Pielet and her second gold ball is No. 8 seed Eryn Cayetano (Corona, Calif.), who produced a comprehensive 6-1, 6-2 result over ninth seed Valencia Xu (Livingston, N.J.).

Things have not gotten any easier for boy’s 18s No. 12 seed Bradley Frye (Overland Park, Kan.) since he toppled the draw’s first seed in the round of 16. His latest victory came over fourth seed Jared Pratt (Daniel Island, S.C.). It was Pratt who got off to a solid start, winning the first set 6-3. The lead was short lived, however, thanks to Frye racing through the second frame 6-1. Both players looked to position themselves to claim the decisive third, which was extended to a tiebreak, where Frye prevailed seven points to four. Across the net from Frye in the final will be Christian Alshon (Boca Raton, Fla.) after the No. 16 seed took down Siem Woldeab 6-2, 7-5 on Monday. Alshon has not lost a set in six matches while letting only 37 games slip away overall.

The two girl’s 18s finalists experienced similar matches in their semifinal victories Monday evening. No. 3 seed Sophia Graver (Allenwood, N.J.) clashed with top seed Chelsea Kung (Forth Worth, Texas), falling behind by a set when Kung took the first 6-4. Graver, who had come from a set down in the quarterfinals, was not deterred, rallying off 12 of the final 14 games to advance 4-6, 6-2, 6-0. Fiona Crawley (San Antonio, Texas) and No. 7 seed Anika Yarlagadda (West Bloomfield, Mich.) went to battle in the other semifinal. Yarlagadda won a back-and-forth opening frame 7-5. However, just like Graver, Crawley dug in and allowed her opponent to claim just two more games the rest of the way, advancing 5-7, 6-1, 6-1.

Originally scheduled as a live broadcast, Tennis Channel will air the boys’ and girls’ 18s finals on a tape-delay basis beginning at 3:30 p.m. ET on Tuesday. With the threat of inclement weather in the forecast, those singles finals will now be played earlier in the day.
