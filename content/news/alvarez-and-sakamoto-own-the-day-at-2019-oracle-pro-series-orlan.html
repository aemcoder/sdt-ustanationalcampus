Alvarez and Sakamoto Own the Day

November 16, 2019

style
article-hero

The men’s and women’s doubles titles were collected on Saturday while the matchups for Sunday’s two singles finals were also determined as part of the action at the 2019 Oracle Pro Series Orlando, held on the grounds of the USTA National Campus.

Nicolas Alvarez  (Peru) and Pedro Sakamoto (Brazil) dominated the day on the men’s side, as they both won their singles semifinal contest before teaming up to capture the doubles crown.

The No. 3 seed in the men’s singles draw. Alvarez toppled No. 1 seed Javier Barranco Cosano (Spain) 7-5, 6-4; the first meeting between the two players lasted well over two hours despite wrapping up in straight sets. After they traded breaks midway through the opening frame, Alvarez closed impressively by holding for 6-5 then breaking at love. The second set played out in a similar fashion, with Alvarez securing the win on his third break point.

For Sakamoto, he needed three sets to put away fourth seed Tomas Martin Etcheverry (Argentina). Set one could have gone to either player, but it was the Argentine who pulled it out six games to four. From there it was all Sakamoto, as he dropped just three more games for a 4-6, 6-1, 6-2 result; it was his second win in a row from a set down.

Returning to the court later in the afternoon for the doubles championship, Alvarez and Sakamoto won the final three points of the match to edge out American duo Charlie Emhardt and Alfredo Perez 7-6(3), 4-6, 10-8.

There was very little drama in women’s singles action on Saturday, as the two victors lost a combined five games. No. 1 seed and current WTA Top 100 player Aranxta Rus (Netherlands) played her best match of the week in ousting eighth-seeded Stephanie Wagner (Germany) 6-3, 6-0. She will face unseeded Irina Fetecau (Romania) on Sunday, who impressively brushed aside an in-form Maria Mateas (United States) 6-2, 6-0. It was the first win in straight sets for Fetecau, who has twice been pushed to 7-5 in the third set over the course of the week.

Wagner did bounce back in the women’s doubles final, as she and partner Katherine Fahey (United States) rallied past the third-seeded tandem of Carolina Meligeni Rodgrigues Alves (Brazil) and Renata Zarazua (Mexico) 4-6, 6-2, 10-7. The win marked the first title for either of the former collegiate All-Americans - Wagner at Miami and Fahey at Michigan - at the ITF $25k level.
