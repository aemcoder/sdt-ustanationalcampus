Sunshine State Supremacy on the Line

November 29, 2017

style
article-hero

Four sectional titles are up for grabs this weekend, with men’s and women’s teams from the 6.5 and 8.5 divisions competing as part of the USTA Florida Sectional Championships held at the USTA National Campus in Orlando, Fla.

Friday and Saturday will have teams battle through their flights via round robin play consisting of team dual matches; each flight winner will move on to Sunday. Team dual matches are made up of three doubles contests, where a team must claim two courts to win the overall match.

The 6.5 men’s division features only two flights, where the flight victors will earn championship match berths. In the women’s 6.5 competition, there are four flights, with only the four flight winners will advance to Sunday’s semifinals.

The 8.5 division consists of three flights on both the men’s and women’s sides, meaning the three flight champions plus a wildcard team will earn semifinal berths.

Play will begin at 8 a.m. on the first two days of the tournament, while Sunday’s slate is set to begin at 8:30 a.m. A full listing of the 6.5 flights and match schedule can be viewed here, and the 8.5 division here.

The 2016 USTA Florida 6.5/8.5 Sectional Championships saw four close contests, as all champions won by a 2-1 margin. The 6.5 division saw the Broward County men defeat Hillsborough County and the Escambia County women take down Polk County. On the 8.5 side, the men’s championship tilt featured Leon County besting Sarasota while the Alachua County women’s team defeated Pinellas County.

Established in 1980, USTA League has grown from 13,000 players in a few parts of the country in its first year to more than 900,000 players across the nation today, making it the world’s largest recreational tennis league.

USTA League was established to provide adult recreational players throughout the country with the opportunity to compete against players of similar ability levels. Players participate on teams in a league format, which is administered by the USTA through its 17 sections. The league groups players by using six National Tennis Rating Program (NTRP) levels, ranging from 2.5 (entry) to 5.0 (advanced). USTA League is open to any USTA member 18 years of age or older.
