The 2018 USTA National Doubles Championships, part of the adidas Junior Championship Series, wrapped up play on Saturday afternoon. Highlighting the day were the four divisional finals, where a quartet of pairs staked claim to USTA Gold Balls to close out there time at the USTA National Campus.

Among the champions were two unseeded tandems plus a pair seeded first and another seeded second. Below are the results from those matches.

Girls’ 14s

Nevena Carton/Daria Smetannikov def. No. 4 Elizabeth Tkachenko/Stephanie Yakoff 2-6, 6-0, 6-2

Girls’ 16s

No. 2 Kate Sharabura/Sophie Williams def. No. 1 Sophia Fornaris/Mia Kintiroglou 4-6, 7-5, 7-6(3)

Boys’ 14s

No. 1 John Lasanajak/Cooper Williams def. No. 3 Owen Megargee/Chase Robinson 3-6, 6-1, 7-5

Boys’ 16s

Joshua Miller/Logan Zapp def. No. 4 Alex Finkelstein/Nathan Mao 6-4, 6-3

Unseeded Eastern tandem Nevena Carton and Daria Smetannikov toughed out their third three-set match of the tournament to claim the girls’ 14s title over No. 4 seeds and fellow Eastern pair Elizabeth Tkachenko and Stephanie Yakoff. After each player held serve to start, Tkachenko and Yakoff reeled off the last four games of the set to take it 6-2. In a drastic turn of events, Carton and Smetannikov stormed back in the second, losing a mere nine points in a set they swept through 6-0. They would not relinquish the momentum to start the final set, earning a break and consolidating for a 2-0 advantage. Tkachenko and Yakoff attempted to stay close, but a second break put the match out of reach; Smetannikov served out the win for a 6-2 margin.

In a clash of the No. 1 and No. 2 seeds in the girls’ 16s championship tilt, second-seeded Kate Sharabura and Sophie Williams from Southern outlasted top-seeded Sophia Fornaris (Florida) and Mia Kintiroglou (Eastern) in a marathon 4-6, 7-5, 7-6(3) encounter. Both duos were coming off extended semifinal matches where they had to rally from a set down to prevail. The No. 1 seeds came through the first set by a slim 6-4 margin; however, they were unable to put Sharabura and Williams away in an equally close second frame, with the No. 2 seeds hanging on to take it 7-5. Fittingly, a back-and-forth third set was pushed to a decisive tiebreak, where Sharabura and Williams were on the winning end of the majority of big points.

With neither team having surrendered a set in reaching the final, the battle for the boys’ 14s title was set up to be a tightly-contested affair. Top seeds John Lasanajak (Southern) and Cooper Williams (Eastern) were looking to got off to a quick start versus No. 3 seeds Owen Megargee and Chase Robinson representing Mid-Atlantic. However, Megargee and Robinson earned four breaks of serve throughout the opening frame to take it 6-3. The No. 1 seeds flipped the script in the second, racing out to a 4-0 lead en route to claiming the set 6-1. After trading breaks twice in the decisive third, Lasanajack was able to save a break point for a slim 6-5 lead. That forced Robinson to attempt to serve the match into a final set tiebreak, but instead it was the top-seeded duo breaking serve for the victory.

The two teams who had dominated the field in the boys’ 16s faced off on Saturday. The unseeded Florida pair of Joshua Miller and Logan Zapp was locked in on capping off an impressive run through the draw, with only No. 4 seeds Alex Finkelstein and Nathan Mao of New England standing in their way. Miller and Zapp might not have been a popular pick to reach the championship stage considering Miller had not played a sanctioned event since the 2017 USTA Boys’ 16s National Clay Court Championships, where he earned a Bronze Ball with his third-place finish. Zapp, meanwhile, came in with soaring confidence following his sweep of the singles and doubles titles at the USTA Boys’ 16s National Clay Court Championships earlier this summer. For Finkelstein and Mao, the two had hopes of winning their second USTA National Doubles crown after claiming the 14s title a year ago. The first set could have gone to either team, but it was Miller and Zapp who grabbed it in the last stages, 6-4. Finkelstein and Mao were kept at bay in the second by the Sunshine State pair, as Miller and Zapp surged to victory six games to three.

Additionally, pairs claiming Bronze Balls on the day included No. 1 Liv Hovde and Ava Krug of Florida in the girls’ 14s, Southern’s No. 4 seeds Emma Charney and Brooke Despriet in the girls’ 16s, No. 2 Nicholas Godsick and Aidan Kim from Midwest in the boys’ 16s, and top seeds Connor and Jake Krug of Florida in the boys’ 16s.
