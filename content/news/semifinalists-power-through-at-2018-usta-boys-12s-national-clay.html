Semifinalists Power Through on Friday

July 20, 2018

style
article-hero

Friday at the 2018 USTA Boys’ 12s National Clay Court Championships, part of the USTA adidas Junior Championship Series, was plagued by a rain delay that lasted nearly six hours. The result was a second consecutive day that concluded around 9:00 p.m., highlighted by the singles semifinal matchups being set for Saturday.

In a bit of a stunning turn of events, not only did none of the quarterfinals go three sets, but the four winners lost a combined 14 games. The closest match in terms of score line was top seed Rudy Quan’s (Sacramento, Calif.) 6-3, 6-2 victory over unseeded Maximus Dussault (Leesburg, Va.). The competitors traded breaks early in the opening set before Quan pulled out in front 5-2. Dussault was able to break to close the gap to 5-3, but was broken right back the next game. Quan would earn three more breaks in the second frame en route to the win.

Quan will clash with No. 4 seed Alexander Razeghi (Humble, Texas), who responded impeccably to the less than ideal weather conditions on Friday. After seeing his 2-0 lead quickly evaporate to open the match, Razeghi held serve to inch in front 3-2 versus fifth-seeded Adam Sun (Glendale, Ariz.) before play was suspended. When the match resumed, Razeghi lost just one more game the rest of the way, claiming the contest 6-3, 6-0. Sun attempted to dig into the match briefly in the second, having two break points on Razeghi’s first service game of the set, but instead Razeghi held and never looked back.

In the bottom half of the draw, unseeded Zhengqing Ji (La Jolla, Calif.) brushed aside No. 29 Siddharth Moturi (Monterey, Calif.) 6-2, 6-2 before the rain started to fall. The match started with three hold of serve before Ji took command at 4-1. He lost just five points on serve, two of which came via double faults, which propelled him to claim the set. Moturi immediately presented more of a challenge in the second, forced Ji’s opening service game to 14 points, but Ji still managed to hold. That game seemed to take the wind out of the sails completely for Moturi, as he won a mere two points over the next three games. He did piece together a small rally to move the score from 5-0 to 5-2, but Ji slammed the door shut from there.

In a matchup of contrasting styles, aggressive baseliner and sixth seed Lucas Coriaty (Long Beach, Calif.) hit through the defense of No. 2 Andrew Salu (Sarasota, Fla.). It took a few games for Coriaty to find his game, getting broken at love to slip behind 2-1; however, he broke back immediately then secured an important hold for 3-2 before the suspension of play. When the players returned to court, Coriaty claimed full control, keeping Salu pinned back behind the baseline nearly every point. Wearing down his opposition effectively, Coriaty did not surrender a game following the delay in his 6-2, 6-0 defeat of Salu.

Due to the extended rain delay on Friday, the doubles semifinals were postponed until Saturday at noon. Action picks back up at 8:00 a.m. with the singles semifinals to take the court at 9:00 a.m.

Among the previous singles champions are current pros Donald Young (2001), Jack Sock (2005), and Tommy Paul (2009). Former doubles champions include collegiate head coaches at BYU (Brad Pearce – 1978), Ohio State (Ty Tucker – 1982), and UCF (Bryan Koniecko – 1999) along with MaliVai Washington (1981), Denis Kudla (2004), Jack Sock (2004 and 2005), and Tommy Paul (2009).
