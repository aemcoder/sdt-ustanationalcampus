Competition Whittled Down to the Semis

October 13, 2018

With the second day of action wrapping up at the 2018 USTA League National Adult 18 & Over 2.5/3.5 Championships, hosted by the USTA National Campus, the three divisions have been trimmed down to the semifinals. Notable amongst Sunday’s play will be the 2.5 women from the Robinswood Tennis Center in Bellevue, Wash., going for their second straight national crown.

Additionally, four sections will be vying for two national titles on Sunday – Caribbean, Florida, Midwest, and Southern.

Pacific Northwest (Robinswood Tennis Center – Bellevue, Wash.) was the first of the women’s 2.5 teams to qualify for the semifinals, polishing off a 4-0 record that featured their players winning all 12 courts they contested while dropping just one set and 48 games. That earned them the top seed, where they will go up against New England (Weston Racquet Club – Weston, Conn.), who put together a 3-1 slate with nine court wins. They still needed the tiebreaking procedure to advance over Florida, which went in New England’s favor thanks to eight sets lost compared to nine for Florida.

Midwest (Midtown Athletic Club – Bannockburn, Ill.) was also able to prevail in all four of their duals to advance to Sunday, claiming nine individual matchups for the No. 2 seed. Their opponent will be third seed Caribbean (Puerto Rico), who did stumble in their final dual on Saturday but was far and away the best one-loss squad after 10 court victories and just five sets surrendered.

For the men’s 3.5 competition, Intermountain (Golds Gym – Orem, Utah), Southern (Stillwater – Flowery Branch, Ga.), and Northern California (Independent) easily put themselves into the semifinals with sterling 4-0 efforts. Intermountain just edged out Southern for the top seed, as their seven sets dropped were two better than the nine by Southern; they both won 17 of the 20 courts they contested. Northern California slotted into the third spot with 16 individual matchups claimed.

The No. 4 seed went to Florida (USTA National Campus – Orlando), the best of a quartet of teams which suffered just one loss overall. The determining factor was the number of courts won, with Florida’s 15 being one greater than both Eastern and Caribbean.

Only two women’s 3.5 teams head into Sunday without suffering a loss. Florida (Red Bug Lake Park – Casselberry) collected 15 individual victories to end a perfect 4-0 and take the No. 1 seed, while Caribbean (Puerto Rico) had a little tougher road, but still came out on top on 13 of the 20 courts they contested. Caribbean’s semifinal opponent will be Southern (Fayetteville Athletic – Fayetteville, Ark.), who rallied back from a loss on Friday to move all the way up into the third position on the strength of the same number of individual wins as top seed Florida. Taking a similar path to the final four was Midwest (South Bend Racquet Club – Mishawaka, Ind.), whose 14 court wins moved them just in front of three other squads with 3-1 overall records.

A full listing of each division’s updated standings can be found at the following links: 2.5 women; 3.5 men; 3.5 women.

The semifinals are slated for 7 a.m. with the championship and third-place matches to follow.

Established in 1980, USTA League has grown from 13,000 players in a few parts of the country in its first year to more than 900,000 players across the nation today, making it the world’s largest recreational tennis league.

USTA League was established to provide adult recreational players throughout the country with the opportunity to compete against players of similar ability levels. Players participate on teams in a league format, which is administered by the USTA through its 17 sections. The league groups players by using six National Tennis Rating Program (NTRP) levels, ranging from 2.5 (entry) to 5.0 (advanced). USTA League is open to any USTA member 18 years of age or older.
