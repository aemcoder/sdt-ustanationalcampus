While rain threatened play in the late afternoon, the 2018 Wheelchair Tennis Collegiate Tennis Championships kept it rolling on day two. 

The morning saw singles match play, with the round robin portion of the tournament continuing. 

Chris Herman of the University of Florida, who won all of his matches on day one, kept up his momentum, by defeating University of Alabama’s Shelby Baron, 6-3, 7-5. 

In the Singles B flight, Maude Jacques bounced back from her defeat on day one to clock not one, but two wins, defeating  University of Alabama teammate Alex Saporito, 6-2, 6-4, and made light work of Andrew Lee of Carlsbad, Calif., 6-1, 6-3. 

Rain hit the USTA National Campus late in the afternoon, pushing the tournament to continue on Player Development Indoor Courts. 

It was a historic day on the National Campus, as Lee, joined by doubles partner Jose Estrada took on the doubles titans team of Shelby Baron and Jacques, subsequently being the first wheelchair tennis players to play a match in the brand new, state-of-the-art, Adidas Center for Performance.  

“It was scary, it was nerve-racking,” noted Estrada, a San Diego native. “But it was fun, though. I love the facility; it’s awesome.”

Not only was this the pair’s first time playing on the USTA Player Development indoor courts, but, as the pair are both Calif. residents, it was their first time playing on indoor courts ever. 

“We’re from San Diego, so we don’t have indoor courts,” said Lee. “So this is new for us.”

Estrada and Lee fell to Baron and Jacques in the semifinal match, setting the stage for Baron and Jacques to face Nathan Melnyk from Stony Brook University, and Herman in Sunday’s doubles final.

Match play is expected to begin at 10 a.m. E.T. on Sunday.
