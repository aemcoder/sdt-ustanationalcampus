USTA Florida Combo 18 & Over 5.5/7.5/9.5 Sectional Championships

Final USTA FL League Event of 2017 to Wrap up on Sunday

December 9, 2017

style
article-hero

Bringing a conclusion to the 2017 USTA Florida League season, the Combo 18 & Over 5.5/7.5/9.5 Sectional Championships have completed two days of play at the National Campus. The men’s 5.5 champion was crowned Saturday evening while the remaining five titles will be earned on Sunday.

The men from Leon emerged as the victors in the 5.5 division, compiling a 3-1 team record over the course of Friday and Saturday. Leon overcame a rocky start to the weekend, getting swept by Marion in its opening dual, but responded to defeat Marion 2-1 in the rematch as well as pick up a pair of wins over Alachua.

In women’s 5.5 action, Flight 1 champion Duval will clash with the top finisher from Flight 2, Leon, in Sunday’s final. Duval was the lone squad to finish with a winning record in its three-team flight, sweeping aside both Escambia and Orange/Seminole twice to post a 4-0 team mark. Leon was 3-0 in its flight, taking two of the three courts contested in each dual to advance to the championship match.

The four teams still alive for the men’s 7.5 title are Marion, Polk, Orange/Seminole and SMD. Polk (Flight 2) was the most dominant of the bunch, moving on unscathed in three duals while letting just one court and a total of four sets slip away. Orange/Seminole also went undefeated at 3-0 in Flight 3, dropping one individual matchup in each of those. Polk will face off with Flight 1 representative Marion, which clinched its spot in the semifinals thanks to a sweep of Lee/Charlotte in a must-win match. Orange/Seminole is set to meet SMD, a team that lost more sets and games than Alachua but secured the head-to-head victory when the two teams met to earn the top spot in Flight 4.

The women’s 7.5 semifinals will pit Broward against Duval and Orange/Seminole versus Marion; all four teams enter Sunday undefeated. Broward was a dominant force in Flight 1, winning all nine courts contested while losing a mere three sets and 59 games. Duval managed to survive and advance from Flight 2 despite losing more sets and games than SMD, claiming the head-to-head encounter with SMD via a sweep. Orange/Seminole breezed through Flight 3 without being challenged, giving away just one set and a meager total of 48 games. Marion got through Flight 4, the only five-team flight in the division, with a 4-0 team record. Marion was the only team to finish with a winning record.

Alachua and Okaloosa will continue to battle against each other as the only two teams in the men’s 9.5 division. They are scheduled for a pair of dual matches on Sunday.

Meanwhile both women’s 9.5 finalists earned the right to play for the title thanks to the tiebreaker procedure. Alachua will represent Flight 1 despite ending the round robin portion of the event at 2-2, the same record as both Broward and Brevard. However, Alachua recorded eight individual court wins, two more than Broward and four more than Brevard. Pinellas also found themselves tied with two other teams, South Miami/Dade and South Orange. Pinellas and South Miami/Dade each claimed six court victories while South Orange only earned five. That made the head-to-head result between Pinellas and South Miami/Dade the decider, which was won by Pinellas.

Established in 1980, USTA League has grown from 13,000 players in a few parts of the country in its first year to more than 900,000 players across the nation today, making it the world’s largest recreational tennis league.

USTA League was established to provide adult recreational players throughout the country with the opportunity to compete against players of similar ability levels. Players participate on teams in a league format, which is administered by the USTA through its 17 sections. The league groups players by using six National Tennis Rating Program (NTRP) levels, ranging from 2.5 (entry) to 5.0 (advanced). USTA League is open to any USTA member 18 years of age or older.
