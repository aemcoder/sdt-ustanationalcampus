The first day of play at the 2018 USTA Junior Team Tennis 14 & Under National Championships was extended by a pair of rain delays, eventually wrapping up indoors. In the end, all teams completed two dual matches to keep the event on schedule heading into Saturday.

Using the World Team Tennis format, each team dual match features five individual matchups – boys’ singles, girls’ singles, boys’ doubles, girls’ doubles and mixed doubles.

In the Intermediate division, Midwest (Advantage Indy) has full control of Flight A as the only unbeaten team remaining. The remaining three flights all have two squads tied atop the standings at 2-0. Flight B will be decided by the Eastern (MatchPoint Magic) and Southern (One Hit Wonders) clash on Saturday, while Flight C comes down to a matchup between Texas (Dallas Select) and Caribbean (Caguas II). Finally, in Flight D, the team moving on to the championship semifinals will be either Mid-Atlantic (Westwood Nuclear Nine) or Northern California (Four Directions Tennis).

The Advanced competition has played out in a similar fashion. Flight A belonged to Texas (ATA Dream Team) on the day, closing out Friday at 2-0 to put them in the driver’s seat. After not even surrendering a single set on day one, Northern (SLP Yeti’s) and Southern California (South Bay Bombers) will battle for the top spot in Flight B. Undefeated squads Mid-Atlantic (Linx Warriors) and Southern (Atlanta Smashers) are set to square off for Flight C supremacy. Meanwhile, the Texas Section looks poised to send two teams into the semifinal round thanks to the wild card entrant Dallas Select cruising so far in Flight D; standing in their way is Eastern (NYJTL Team Huber), also unscathed on Friday.

Matches pick back up beginning at 8 a.m. After the completion of the 10:30 am round on Saturday, the tournament transitions to its playoff rounds for the afternoon, with the team champions to be crowned on Sunday.

Both championship duals, taking place on Collegiate courts 1-2 and 7-8, will be broadcast live and can be watched here.

Established in 1991, USTA Junior Team Tennis provides youth with all of the health and social benefits of tennis in a coed, fun and competitive environment – all while allowing participants to compete with and against individuals of similar ages and skill levels.

With more than 85,000 players competing nationwide, USTA Junior Team Tennis brings players together to enjoy camaraderie and teamwork, providing a fun environment for kids to learn that succeeding is really more about how they play the game – win or lose.

Each year the USTA crowns a national champion in two age divisions (14U and 18U) in both the Intermediate and Advanced classifications. In last year’s 14U competition, the OC Dream Team representing the Southern California Section brought home the Advanced crown while Northern California’s Cal Tennis Club The Council captured the Intermediate title.
