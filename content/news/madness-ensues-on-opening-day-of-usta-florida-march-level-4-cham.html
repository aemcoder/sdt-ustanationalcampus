Madness Ensues on Opening Day

March 17, 2018

style
article-hero

Results from the opening day of the USTA Florida March Level 4 Championships proved that madness in the month of March is not reserved solely for the hardwood. Of the tournament’s eight draws, a total of five had at least half the seeds knocked out of the main draw on Saturday.

The girls’ 16s and 18s draws featured the largest amount of surprises, headlined by No. 1 seeds Kassandra Di Staulo and Casey Accola crashing out early. Things could not have been more unpredictable in the girls’ 16s, where four seeds were taken down in the first round while the other four lost in the round of 16, leaving zero seeds in the quarterfinals of the main draw. Results held just slightly more true to form in the girls’ 18s, with three seeds amongst the final eight.

Half of the seeds are still in the hunt for the title in the girls’ 14s division, with top-seeded Jasmin Makela surviving a test in her first round matchup, prevailing in a match tiebreak by a 10 points to six margin. Just two seeds will be missing from the girls’ 12s main draw when play picks back up on Sunday. Of those remaining, only No. 8 Mary Boyce Deatherage has surrendered a set; top seed Catherine Goldman was pushed in the second frame of her opening round, coming away with a 6-3, 7-5 victory.

It was a much calmer day of action for the competitors on the boys’ side, as all four top seeds did successfully navigate their way into the quarterfinals. But just as with the girls, the older divisions delivered more upsets.

The 16s and 18s have exactly half of their seeds left in the main draw.  Boys’ 16s No. 1 seed Chase Martin needed a tiebreak to secure the first set he contested on the day, but dropped just three games across the other three sets he played. Meanwhile, second seed Ben Shelton and No. 3 seed Johan Braswell both outlasted their second round opponent in a decisive match tiebreak.

Players seeded first, second and third also moved into the quarterfinals in the boys’ 18s division. Top seed Matthew Fung plus No. 2 seed Matthew Segura were extended to match tiebreaks in the round of 16; third-seeded Logan Zapp came out on top in a pair of straight-set encounters on the day.

Just one seed, No. 7 Alexander Di Staulo, was defeated on Saturday in the boys’ 12s draw. It was relatively smooth sailing for the rest. Top seed Yubel Ubri and third seed Nathan Blokhin were the most dominant, giving away just five and four games, respectively. The boys’ 14s lost two seeds (No. 3 and No. 4) during the first day while second-seed Yair Gritzman was pushed to the brink right out of the gate, holding off his opening round opponent for a 6-3, 5-7, 10-8 triumph. Top seed Yannik Rahman got through with two comfortable wins in straight sets.
