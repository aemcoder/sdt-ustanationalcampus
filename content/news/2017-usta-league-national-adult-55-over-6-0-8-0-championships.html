USTA League National Adult 55 & Over 6.0/8.0 Championships

Teams to Compete for National Titles Sunday

October 28, 2017

style
article-hero

With temperatures finally cooling off in the state of Florida, play heated up on Friday and Saturday at the 2017 USTA League National Adult 55 & Over 6.0/8.0 Championships, held at the USTA National Campus in Orlando, Fla. Two days of round robin action in the 15 total flights produced four semifinalists in each division.

In the 6.0 women’s competition, Florida will be up against Northern and Northern California is pitted against Pacific Northwest in the semifinals. The Sunshine State representatives dominated Flight 9, the only flight with five squads. They posted a perfect 4-0 team record thanks to picking up victories on nine of the 12 individual courts they contested, giving away only eight sets in all. Northern also went undefeated in Flight 11, with their head-to-head win over Caribbean proving to be the decisive dual triumph.

Meanwhile Northern California escaped Flight 10 unscathed behind seven wins and two losses in the individual matchups they competed in over the two days. In much more dramatic fashion, Pacific Northwest came through Flight 12 thanks to the tiebreak procedure. The squad from Seattle finished with a 2-1 record overall, as did Mid-Atlantic and Eastern. However, Pacific Northwest claimed the flight after ending with a better individual matchup record (6-3) than the other two (5-4).

For the men’s 6.0 division, Southern California, Southern, and Texas all claimed their respective flights to secure a semifinal berth while Northern rounded out the final four via the wild card selection. Southern California and Northern were the class of Flight 13, as they both swept the other three teams in the flight. The squad from the Golden State claimed the dual match with the representative from the Twin Cities to automatically advance; Northern moved on with the best winning percentage of all the second-place finishers.

Southern California’s semifinal opponent will be the Southern, a team that dominated Flight 14 behind a mere 5 sets and 56 games lost. Northern and Texas are in the other semifinal pairing after Texas cruised through Flight 15, giving away only four sets and 65 games.

Florida, Hawaii, Eastern, and Mid-Atlantic head into Sunday as the four teams still alive for the women’s 8.0 crown. Florida was the lone team to post an undefeated dual record amongst the 17 in the division. They breezed past the competition in Flight 1 behind 10 individual court victories compared to just two defeats; their eight sets lost are the fewest amongst the semifinalists despite having played one more team match than the other three squads.

Eastern will be across the net from Florida in one semifinal after emerging from Flight 3 via their six individual court victories and head-to-head win over Pacific Northwest. Hawaii stayed in the championship hunt in a similar fashion, closing out Saturday with the same individual matchup record (5-4) and sets lost (10) as Southern but owned the head-to-head tiebreaker. Their semifinal opponent, Mid-Atlantic, was one of three squads with a 2-1 team record in Flight 4. They eventually were named the flight champion thanks to winning six courts to Northern California’s five and a defeat of Northern.

All four men’s 8.0 semifinalists – Mid-Atlantic, Southern, Southern California and Middle States – went undefeated over the first two days of the event. Mid-Atlantic was not tested in Flight 5, going 4-for-4 in duals while only surrendering two individual courts and seven sets; the other four teams in the flight lost at least six courts and 13 sets. Mid-Atlantic will take on the Southern California representative, which got through Flight 7 with ease after taking seven individual matchups.

Southern was not to be stopped in Flight 2, also winning seven of the nine courts they contested against their trio of opponents. Even more dominant was their semifinal opponent, Middle States, a team that claimed eight court victories and let just two sets slip out of their grasp in Flight 4.

Sunday’s semifinals will take the courts at 7:30 a.m. with the championship matches to follow.

Established in 1980, USTA League has grown from 13,000 players in a few parts of the country in its first year to more than 900,000 players across the nation today, making it the world’s largest recreational tennis league.

USTA League was established to provide adult recreational players throughout the country with the opportunity to compete against players of similar ability levels. Players participate on teams in a league format, which is administered by the USTA through its 17 sections. The league groups players by using six National Tennis Rating Program (NTRP) levels, ranging from 2.5 (entry) to 5.0 (advanced). USTA League is open to any USTA member 18 years of age or older.
