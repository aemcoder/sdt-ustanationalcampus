Campus Reopens Its Doors

USTA Staff | July 27, 2020

style
article-hero

It was an exciting moment when the USTA National Campus officially welcomed back players on June 8. Thanks to following the Playing Tennis Safely guidelines, guests immediately started enjoying our sport again in Lake Nona. 

"We have reopened the doors to the USTA National Campus and seen a steady rebuild of the program,” said Simon Gale, USTA National Campus Director of Tennis. “We have just started our junior summer program and sold out the first two weeks. We are thrilled to see adult and junior players back on Campus enjoying our wonderful sport. In addition to the players returning we have welcomed back our teaching staff. After talking about what we want to do on court for three months, it's so great to finally see them interacting with the players again. Our first week back was very encouraging with close to capacity adult programming and the first two weeks of junior programming sold out. 

"The Campus has taken a slow and steady approach to reopening. We have placed a heavy emphasis on both staff and player safety as they return to the courts. Precautions include wearing masks, closing every other court, hand sanitizing stations and making sure all customers that enter the building have completed both a health questionnaire and a temperature check. By taking these measures we aim to provide the safest environment possible." 

Although players and teaching pros were unable to hit the National Campus courts from mid-March to early-June, everyone is ready to create even more memories. 

"During the period we were closed there was a lot of talk about how great tennis is for people and how it is a natural for social distancing,” said Gale. “Tennis has the ability to change peoples’ lives so it is more than just tennis that we do here. The sport naturally brings people together and creates new friendships while you get a tremendous workout. You only have to ask someone ‘Why tennis and why the National Campus,’ and the answers speak for themselves."
