Boys’ No. 1 Seeds Toppled, Girls' No. 1 Seeds Survive

December 30, 2017

style
article-hero

Following the completion of play Saturday at the 2017 USTA National Winter Championships for the 16s and 18s age divisions, hosted by the USTA National Campus, the four singles draws sit at the quarterfinal round while the four doubles draws are down to the semifinals.

After three days of easy victories, all four singles top seeds found themselves in trouble on Saturday. While girls’ 16s No. 1 seed Gianna Pielet (El Paso, Texas) and girls’ No. 1 seed Chelsea Kung (Fort Worth, Texas) survived three-set contests, the first seeds in the two boys’ draws were not as fortunate.

Coming in as one of the No. 17 seeds in the boys’ 16s draw, JJ Tracy (Hilton Head, S.C.) toughed out a lengthy first set seven points to four in a tiebreak and ran through the second 6-2 to stun top-seeded Maxwell McKennon (Newport Beach, Calif.). McKennon had lost just 14 games in his three prior matches. That result leaves the draw wide open, as No. 6 seed Harsh Parikh (Tucson, Ariz.) is the highest remaining; all other quarterfinalists are seeded with the exception of Zachary Smith (New Orleans, La.).

Carson Haskins (Ballwin, Mo.), who earned top billing in the boys’ 18s draw, also had his path to title derailed on Saturday. Going up against No. 12 Bradley Frye (Overland Park, Kan.) in the round of 16, it was Frye who took control and held on for the 6-3, 6-4 victory. While that does leave the draw without its top two seeds, No. 3 Kevin Zhu (Pearland, Texas), fourth seed Jared Pratt (Daniel Island, S.C.) and No. 5 Ryan Goetz (Greenlawn, N.Y.) have all advanced to the final eight. Unseeded Noah Schachter (Wesley Chapel, Fla.) and Andres Martin (Flowery Branch, Ga.) also continued their unexpected runs this week.

The most turbulent of the draws since the start has been the girls’ 16s, and another major upset was mere points away from happening on day four. Pielet was up against unseeded Leyden Games (Newport Beach, Calif.), with Games quickly taking a set advantage after winning the opening frame 6-2. Pielet responded well, winning the second 6-2, but the final set could have gone either way. It was eventually decided in a tiebreak, where Pielet prevailed seven points to four. The draw still features a trio of unseeded hopefuls in Allura Zamarripa (Saint Helena, Calif.), Justine Dondonay (Alhambra, Calif.) and Eleana Yu (Mason, Ohio).

Girls’ 18s No. 1 Kung also found herself down a set against tenth seed Melissa Sakar (Lauderdale Lakes, Fla.), who claimed the first 6-3. Kung was able to navigate through a tight second frame to take it 6-4 and was much more authoritative in the final frame, breezing through it 6-1. She will be joined by No. 3 seed Sophia Graver (Allenwood, N.J.) and seventh-seeded Anika Yarlagadda (West Bloomfield, Mich.) as the top eight seeds remaining; unseeded Elizabeth Coleman (Midland, Mich.) has also advanced to the quarterfinals.

An unseeded finalist is guaranteed in the boys’ 16 doubles competition, with Matthew Mu (La Jolla, Calif.) and Scott Sculley (Hilton Head Island, S.C.) facing off with Jeremie Casabon (Flourtown, Pa.) and Holden Koons (York, Pa.) in one semifinal. The other semifinal will feature No. 9 seeds Michael Andre (Las Vegas, Nev.) and Blake Kasday (Las Vegas, Nev.) versus Keshav Chopra (Marietta, Ga.) and Coy Simon (Charleston, S.C.). The final four of the boys’ 18s doubles draw consists of eighth seeds Leighton Allen (Austin, Texas) and Zhu, the No. 10 duo of Phuc Huynh (Honolulu, Hawaii) and Andre Ilagan (Honolulu, Hawaii), 11th-seeded Phillip Jordan (Spartanburg, S.C.) and Martin plus the unseeded team of Alexander Kotzen (Short Hills, N.J.) and Patrick Maloney (Oyster Bay, N.Y.).

The top half of the girls’ 16s doubles draw held true to form, as first seeds Carmen Corley (Albuquerque, N.M.) and Pielet are set to battle No. 4 seeds Maribella and Allura Zamarripa (Saint Helena, Calif.); both duos outlasted their quarterfinal opponents in extended match tiebreaks. In the bottom half, the 12th seeds Ava Catanzarite (Pittsburgh, Pa.) and Lana Mavor (Yarmouth, Maine) will be across the net from No. 15 seeds Dondonay and Anessa Lee (San Marino, Calif.). The No. 1 seeds in the girls’ 18s, Kolie Allen (Lombard, Ill.) and Ivana Corley (Albuquerque, N.M.), continued their winning ways, still yet to drop a set en route to the semifinals. Next up for them is the upset-minded Coleman and Emma Jackson (La Grange Park, Ill.), while third seeds Clarissa and Christina hand (Moorestown, N.J.) will play No. 6 seeds Dasha Kourkina (Brooklyn, N.Y.) and Michelle Sorokko (Little Neck, N.Y.) for a place in the championship.
