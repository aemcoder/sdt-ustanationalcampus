The final USTA League National Championship for 2018 got underway Friday at the USTA National Campus, as teams vying for titles in the Mixed 40 & Over 6.0/7.0/8.0/9.0 divisions were on court. All teams have two dual matches under their belt going into the second day of competition.

In a change to this year’s format, teams will compete in a total of four unflighted matches against randomly selected opponents. Every team will play two dual matches on Friday and two more on Saturday. On Sunday, the top four teams (based on TennisLink standings) will advance to the semifinals with the championship and third-place matches to follow.

Below are the teams that will enter Saturday’s schedule of play still unbeaten after post a pair of victories to open the weekend.

6.0 teams: Caribbean (Independent – Puerto Rico), Midwest (Kettering Tennis Center – Kettering, Ohio), Missouri Valley (Lafortune Tennis Center – Tulsa, Okla.),  Northern (Life Time Fitness-Bloomington South – Bloomington, Minn.), Pacific Northwest (Eastside Tennis Center – Kirkland, Wash.), Southern (Cedar Bluff Racquet Club – Knoxville, Tenn.)

7.0 teams: Caribbean (Independent – Puerto Rico), Hawaii Pacific (Keehi Lagoon – Honolulu), Middle States (Nassau Racquet & Tennis Club – Skillman, N.J.), Pacific Northwest (Amy Yee Tennis Center – Seattle), Southern (Germantown Country Club – Memphis, Tenn.)

8.0 teams: Intermountain (Independent – Nevada), Midwest (Western Reserve Racquet and Fitness Club – Streetsboro, Ohio), Missouri Valley (Aspen Athletic Club – Des Moines, Iowa), Southern (City of Benton – Benton, Ark.), Southern California (Whittier College Richard Ettinger Tennis Center – Whittier)

9.0 teams: Eastern (Deer Park Tennis Center Inc. – Deer Park, N.Y.), Mid-Atlantic (Independent – Montgomery County, Md.), Missouri Valley (Lafortune Tennis Center – Tulsa, Okla.)

Action continues at 7 a.m. Saturday morning. A full listing of teams competing in each division can be found at the following links: 6.0 division; 7.0 division; 8.0 division; 9.0 division.

Established in 1980, USTA League has grown from 13,000 players in a few parts of the country in its first year to more than 900,000 players across the nation today, making it the world’s largest recreational tennis league.

USTA League was established to provide adult recreational players throughout the country with the opportunity to compete against players of similar ability levels. Players participate on teams in a league format, which is administered by the USTA through its 17 sections. The league groups players by using six National Tennis Rating Program (NTRP) levels, ranging from 2.5 (entry) to 5.0 (advanced). USTA League is open to any USTA member 18 years of age or older.
