Singles Semifinals, Doubles Finals to be Contested Monday

January 1, 2018

style
article-hero

Five days are now in the books at the 2017 USTA National Winter Championships for the 16s and 18s age divisions, hosted by the USTA National Campus. Monday will see a quartet of doubles teams earn titles as well as players secure their place in the singles championships scheduled for Tuesday.

The main draw singles semifinals will hit the courts at 10 a.m. Monday morning, while the doubles finals will be contested in the afternoon.

Fresh off his upset of the boys’ 16s top seed, JJ Tracy (Hilton Head, S.C.) got off to a hot start against another of the No. 17 seeds, Niraj Komatineni (Mason, Ohio). But after winning the opening set 6-2, Komatineni stormed back to sweep the second 6-0. Tracy was able to rebound, running away with the final frame 6-2. Next up for him is eighth-seeded Keshav Chopra (Marietta, Ga.), who came through a tight two-set contest by the margin of 6-4, 7-5 against Aditya Gupta (Irvine, Calif.). In the other semifinal, No. 6 seed Harsh Parikh (Tucson, Ariz.) will take on ninth seed Logan Zapp (Fleming Island, Fla.) after Parikh survived a three-set battle with Zachary Smith (New Orleans, La.) and Zapp cruised past Daniel Milavsky (Needham, Mass.) 6-1, 6-1.

Also on Sunday, No. 12 seed Bradley Frye (Overland Park, Kan.) backed up his upset of the boys’ 18s top seed when he took down fifth seeds Ryan Goetz (Greenlawn, N.Y.) 6-4, 7-6(1). His semifinal opponent will be fourth seed Jared Pratt (Daniel Island, S.C.), who claimed a marathon 6-3, 4-6, 7-6(5) contest with unseeded Noah Schachter (Wesley Chapel, Fla.). Meanwhile, No. 16 seed Christian Alshon (Boca Raton, Fla.) has yet to drop a set en route to the semifinal stage, most recently defeating third-seeded Kevin Zhu (Pearland, Texas) 6-4, 6-1. Awaiting him on Monday will be Siem Woldeab (La Mesa, Calif.) following his lengthy 7-5, 3-6, 6-4 victory over unseeded Andres Martin (Flowery Branch, Ga.).

Girls’ 16s No. 1 seed Gianna Pielet (El Paso, Texas) was on the brink of elimination for the second day in a row, but managed to come out on top of her matchup with 10th seed Rose Garcia Gross (New York, N.Y.) that lasted over three and a half hours, 6-4, 5-7, 6-4. Next up for her is unseeded Allura Zamarripa (Saint Helena, Calif.), who was impressive in defeating No. 11 Carmen Corley (Albuquerque, N.M.) in straight sets. The bottom half of the draw features eight seed Eryn Cayetano (Corona, Calif.) and No. 9 seed Valencia Xu (Livingston, N.J.) after the two handled unseeded opponents on Sunday.

A day after being pushed to the limit, girls’ 18s top seed Chelsea Kung (Fort Worth, Texas) recorded a 7-5, 6-4 triumph over Clarissa Hand (Moorestown, N.J.) to reach the semifinals. The other three winners in the girls’ 18s draw needed three sets. No. 3 seed Sophia Graver (Allenwood, N.J.), Kung’s next opponent, toughed out a 2-6, 6-2, 7-5 result against unseeded Elizabeth Coleman (Midland, Mich.). Seventh seed Anika Yarlagadda (West Bloomfield, Mich.) outplayed 16th seed Angelica Blake (Boca Raton, Fla.) in their decisive set to claim the match 7-5, 4-6, 6-3, while her semifinal foe Fiona Crawley (San Antonio, Texas) swept No. 15 Dasha Kourkina (Brooklyn, N.Y.) in the final frame of their contest.

Monday will see four doubles national champions crowned. In the boys’ 16s competition, unseeded duo Matthew Mu (La Jolla, Calif.) and Scott Sculley (Hilton Head Island, S.C.) are set for a matchup with No. 5 seeds Chopra and Coy Simon (Charleston, S.C.).The boys’ 18s draw will also feature a seeded versus unseeded championship. The 11th-seeded tandem of Phillip Jordan (Spartanburg, S.C.) and Martin, who earlier in the tournament upset the top seeds, held on for victory in a tense 7-6(3), 6-7(8), 6-4 on Sunday; they will meet Alexander Kotzen (Short Hills, N.J.) and Patrick Maloney (Oyster Bay, N.Y.) for the title.

Pielet and Corley, the first seeds in the girls’ 16s, shook off a slow start in their semifinal showdown with No. 4 seeds Allura and Maribella Zamarripa (Saint Helena, Calif.) to win 6-3, 6-4. Awaiting them in the final is the 15th-seeded pair of Justine Dondonay (Alhambra, Calif.) and Anessa Lee (San Marino, Calif.), who rallied from a set down on Sunday. A dominant force the entire tournament, girls’ 18s No. 1 seeds Kolie Allen (Lombard, Ill.) and Ivana Corley (Albuquerque, N.M.) still have not surrendered a set in reaching the final; the most games they have lost in a match is seven. Sisters Clarissa and Christina Hand (Moorestown, N.J.), seeded third, now stand in their way after winning a three-set battle in the semifinals.
