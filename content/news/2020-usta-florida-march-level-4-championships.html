2020 USTA Florida March Level 4 Championships

Junior Sectional Play Returns

March 12, 2020

style
article-hero

A staple of the junior event calendar since the inaugural year of the USTA National Campus, the USTA Florida March Level 4 Championships is set to make its return this weekend. The tournament will feature some of the Sunshine State’s best competitors in the boys’ and girls’ 16s & 18s singles divisions.

First matches are slated to take to the courts at 8 a.m. Saturday, March 14, with play running continuing until the afternoon of Monday, March 16. The four 32-player draws consist of a feed-in consolation through the round of 16. All matches utilize regular scoring and be the best two-of-three tiebreak sets with a 10-point match tiebreak in lieu of a full third.

Leading the boys’ 18s field is No. 1 seed and Florida State commit Maks Silagy (Bradenton), who bounced back from an opening round loss to take home the consolation title in 2019. Andrew Pereverzev (Hallandale Beach), the boys’ 16s consolation champion from a year ago, has earned the No. 2 seed overall, while Emilio Van Cotthem (Fort Pierce) is seeded third and William Mroz (Bradenton) checks in as the fourth seed. Van Cotthem will be looking for a much better performance after he lost both of his matches at last year’s event; Mroz, a quarterfinalist in 2019, is coming off a tournament win in February at the USTA Florida Orange County Level 5 hosted by Lake Cane.

Boys’ 16s top seed Phillip Dell (Bradenton) went a disappointing 0-2 in the same division last year as an unseeded competitor; however, his most recent singles appearance at the National Campus was much more successful, as he captured the boys’ 14s crown at the USTA Florida “Bobby Curtis” Junior Sectional Championships in June. No. 2 seed Jonathan Irwanto (Miami Shores) has also vaulted up the rankings since being unseeded and posting an 0-2 record a year ago. Third-seeded Max Lindstrom (Satellite Beach) went 2-2 in the 16s draw in 2019, but is coming off playing his way to raising the trophy in the boys’ 16s at the USTA Florida Seminole County Level 5. Jose Murariu (Lakewood Ranch), the No. 4 seed, did not compete in the event last year but has reached the quarterfinals in his previous two appearances at the National Campus.

Maria Drobotova (Deerfield Beach) has reeled in match wins consistently over the past 12 months, going from being unseeded in the girls’ 16s last year all the way to the No. 1 seed in the girls’ 18s draw this year. Second seed Valeria Ray (Doral) is playing up in the 18s following a highly successful run in the 14s, which included bringing home a Silver Ball in both singles and doubles during the 2019 season. No. 3 Emma Fernald (Saint Petersburg) was actually upset by Drobotova in the opening round of last year’s tournament; fourth-seeded Anna Babayan (Bradenton) was the consolation runner-up in the 16s division following a first round defeat.

No. 1 seed Sophie Llewellyn (Tarpon Springs) collected four victories in reaching the championship match of the 14s draw in 2019, where she fell to this year’s fourth-seeded Lexington Reed (Orlando) 4-6, 7-5, 10-6. No. 2 seed Makenna Doyle (Boca Raton) had a pair of disappointing showings at the National Campus last year, going a combined 1-4 in the USTA Florida Level 4 and the “Bobby Curtis” Junior Sectional Championships; she will instead look to channel the form she showed at a USTA National Level 2 event last May, in which she placed fourth in singles and second in doubles. Third-seeded Akasha Urhobo (Fort Lauderdale) was last year’s consolation champion in the 16s draw.

At last year’s event, No. 1 seeds Anuj Watane and Yannik Rahman claimed the boys’ 18s and boys’ 16s titles, respectively. In the girls’ draws, top-seeded Sofia Rojas was crowned the champion in the 18s while unseeded Hope Moulin played her way to victory in the 16s.
