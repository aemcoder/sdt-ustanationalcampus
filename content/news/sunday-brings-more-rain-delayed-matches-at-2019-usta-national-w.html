Another set of multiple rain delays at the USTA National Campus was unfortunately the major story for day two of the 2019 USTA National Winter Championships, part of the USTA adidas Junior Championship Series. Among the cancellations on the day were all boys’ 16s singles matches along with the boys’ 16s doubles plus the girls’ 16s and 18s doubles.

The top eight competitors in the boys’ 18s division safely advanced to the round of 32. Included in those results were easy victories for No. 1 Logan Zapp (Fleming Island, Fla.) and No. 2 Leighton Allen (Austin, Texas), while third-seeded Welsh Hotard (New Orleans) suffered a letdown in the second set of his match against Rohan Reddy (Glen Head, N.Y.) before prevailing 6-0, 5-7, 6-1; all of the other players among the top eight won in straight sets.

Sunday did bring a few surprises in boys’ 18s action with losses by No. 9 William Cooksey (Grosse Pointe Shores, Mich.) and 10th seed Daniel Milavsky (Needham, Mass.). Cooksey was bested by Nathan Han (Tulsa, Okla.) 6-4, 6-2 while Milavsky could not complete the comeback against Brandon McKinney (Marietta, Ga.), falling 6-3, 3-6, 6-3. Additionally, Bruno Krenn (Calabasas, Calif.) backed up his opening round upset by taking care of Riley Odell (Plano, Texas) 6-4, 6-3. In total, just eight unseeded hopefuls remain in the main draw.

The day also saw the girls’ 16s division finally get in on the action with their opening round of singles, in which the bottom quarter of the top half of the draw severely opened up thanks to numerous upsets. Chief among them was third-seeded Ria Bhakta (Saratoga, Calif.) losing to Lindsey Hofflander (Las Vegas) by a 6-2, 7-5 score line; sixth seed Anna Neyestani (Scottsdale, Ariz.) was another top-ranked player failing to make it out of the opening round thanks to an outstanding performance by Abby Nugent (Springboro, Ohio), who won 6-2, 6-1. No. 10 Tomi Main (Seaside, Calif.) is now the highest remaining seed in the quarter.

For No. 4 seed Kida Ferrari (Fairhope, Ala.), a sluggish start was not enough to derail her from claiming the 2-6, 6-1, 6-0 victory in a matchup with Santianna Chrysopoulo (San Antonio). Second seed Kaite Codd (Carlsbad, Calif.) was eager to finally get her tournament officially underway, breezing past Kennedy Buntrock (Henderson, Nev.) 6-2, 6-2.

A number of players in the girls’ 18s pulled double duty on day two, having to complete their opening round matches before stepping back out on court for a second round contest. Notable results included No. 1 Sarah Hamner (Fort Collins, Colo.) dropping an extended tiebreak in the first set versus Makenna Thiel (Piedmont, Calif.) before rallying for a 6-7(9), 6-3, 6-0 triumph and Ujvala Jupalli (Parsippany, N.J.), who stunned No. 3 Julia Fliegner on Saturday, falling 6-1, 6-4 to Muskan Mahajan (San Ramon, Calif.).

Second seed Neha Velaga (Colmar, Pa.) and No. 4 Gianna Pielet (El Paso, Texas) recorded a pair of easy victories to move into the round of 32, while No. 10 Leyden Games (Irvine, Calif.) was the beneficiary of a retirement from Nadejda Maslova (Brooklyn, N.Y.) with the score standing at 4-6, 3-0. The highest seed to be relegated to the consolation draw was No. 16 Mirabelle Brettkelly (San Francisco) after a 6-2, 6-4 setback against Jackeline Lopez (Lawrenceville, Ga.). Lopez is one of nine unseeded players still competing for the Gold Ball.

The boys’ 18s division is the only of the four to have completed a round of doubles after two days of the event, but that was done so only after shortening the format; all matches were played using short sets to four with a tiebreak at 4-4.

While the format adjustment could have impacted the number of matches that split sets, in the end the vast majority of the favored duos came through. While the top three teams received a bye, the fifth-seeded tandem of Milavsky and Joshua Raab (Woodstock, Ga.) were edged out by Han and fellow Tulsa native Graydon Lair 4-2, 0-4, 10-4. No. 4 Keshav Chopra (Marietta, Ga.) and Coy Simon (Charleston, S.C.) barely avoided a loss, securing a 4-0, 2-4, 10-5 win over Juandedios Abboud (Laredo, Texas) and Gabriel Castillo-Sanchez (Washingtonville, N.Y.).
