The 2018 USTA Junior Team Tennis 18 & Under National Championships, held at the USTA National Campus, wrapped up its opening day of play on a warm Friday afternoon in Orlando. All teams competed in two dual matches over the course of the day as the Intermediate and Advanced flights began to take shape.

For the Intermediate competition, Middle States (Delaware Nightmare) and Southern California (Supreme Aces) have firm control of Flight C and D, respectively. Despite going 2-0, Middle States won those duals by a combined eight games, meaning they still need to defeat 1-1 Midwest (RCC) to advance to the championship semifinals. Southern California came up with a clutch 49-47 defeat of Texas (ATA Knightmares), which could prove to be the difference if both squads win their final group play dual Saturday.

Also in the Intermediate division, Flight A will come down to the clash between Mid-Atlantic (Metro Aces) and Pacific Northwest (Aces) after each finished Friday unscathed behind eight individual matchup victories and a total of 112 games won. Flight B will be decided when Northern California (Laguna Creek Coed) and Northern (BLS) meet; the flight’s two undefeated teams ended day one with a total of eight courts and 17 sets collected each to set up their showdown.

Friday’s Advanced action saw Southern (Angels) dominate Flight C, winning both duals on the strength of eight individual matchup victories, including 16 sets wins compared to just four sets surrendered. The other three flights will come down to battles between teams with 2-0 records. Midwest (RCC) gave away one lone court and a mere three sets in Flight A, but still needs to get past Eastern (Sportime Acers) on Saturday. Flight B will feature a seemingly even Northern (SLP McCoy) versus Southwest (Arizona Heat) dual, while Flight D will be captured by either Texas (ATA Dream Team) or Caribbean (Torrimar Hurricanes).

Play continues Saturday morning at 8 a.m. Following the completion of all group play matches in the morning, the tournament switches over to its playoff rounds for the afternoon.

Both championship duals, taking place Sunday at 8 a.m., will be broadcast live and can be watched here.

Established in 1991, USTA Junior Team Tennis provides youth with all of the health and social benefits of tennis in a coed, fun and competitive environment – all while allowing participants to compete with and against individuals of similar ages and skill levels.

With more than 85,000 players competing nationwide, USTA Junior Team Tennis brings players together to enjoy camaraderie and teamwork, providing a fun environment for kids to learn that succeeding is really more about how they play the game – win or lose.
