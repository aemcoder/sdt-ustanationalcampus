We are thrilled to have tennis expert, Kathy Woods, as part of the USTA National Campus team! From serving as a USPTA president to training countless providers on how to teach the game, Kathy Woods is now the Director of Tennis of the USTA National Campus where she will run innovative and engaging programming for all ages and abilities.

Kathy's tips below will help get your mind and body ready for your next match!

BODY

Warm up your body prior to play by stretching gently before you get to the court.

Keep your feet moving to enable you to get into the best position for striking the ball – waist-high balls will allow you to strike offensively.

Remember to breathe slowly and deeply to help calm your nerves and reduce your heart rate.

Drink plenty of fluids and add electrolytes into the mix to hydrate so you can give your best physical effort.

ERROR REDUCTION

Reduce unforced errors by matching up your swing speed to the difficulty of the ball you are playing. Difficult balls: slower swings; comfortable balls: faster swings.

Aim for targets that allow room for error on the sideline and baseline boundaries. Risk only one line at a time for the best chance at a successful shot.

ATTITUDE

Cooperate with your opponent in the warm-up by hitting balls as accurately to him or her as possible. This is not the time to practice your “put-away” shots.

You should remain serious and focused as you play – yet positive in spirit and attitude. Good sporting behavior is expected in your communication with your opponent.  Let your tennis shots speak for you!

TACTICS

As the match progresses, think about what shot placements and patterns of play best expose your opponent’s weakness. If your weaknesses are being exposed, try to figure out what shots you may be delivering that are contributing to your opponent’s success and your detriment. 

For example: In doubles, if you hit lots of deep high balls, your partner may be lobbed more frequently. If you hit down the line in singles, you will do more running than your opponent.
