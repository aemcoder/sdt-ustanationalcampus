Teams Battle in Flight Play on Saturday

Saturday marked the start of the 2018 USTA Florida Mixed 40 & Over 6.0/8.0 Sectional Championships, kicking off a busy summer of USTA Florida Adult League events held at the National Campus. Each team completed a pair of dual matches on the day, as the divisional flights began to take shape.

In the 6.0 competition, Duval (Julington Creek Plantation) looks poised to move on to the championship match after going 2-0 on Saturday. With Flight 1 being composed of just three teams, Duval only needs to defeat 1-1 Escambia (Wilson) on Sunday to clinch the flight. IRMSL (Legacy), who sits at 0-2, remains alive mathematically, but will need to win twice Sunday. Flight 2 saw Volusia (FTC) jump to the top of the standings after day one. With wins over Marion (FK) and Leon, Volusia can lock up its spot in the final by defeating Alachua (DB) Sunday. Marion and Alachua sit at 1-1 overall, meaning there is still much on the line for both squads.

The 8.0 division contains three flights, meaning teams are seeking to advance to the semifinals via finishing at the top of their respective flight or the one wild card spot. It appears that Flight 1 will come down to the victor between Pinellas (SPTC) and Broward (Midtown). Both squads bring 2-0 overall records into Sunday, when they will face off at noon. Polk (Lakes Wales) also stayed in the mix by going 1-1 on opening day. Flight 2 presents a similar situation, with both SMD (Coral Oaks) and Okaloosa (Shalimar) posting 2-0 records on Saturday; they are set to battle at 8:00 a.m. Sunday. Additionally, Lee/Charlotte (Vivante) finished day one at 1-1 and will look to win twice Sunday in hopes of moving on the semifinal round. In Flight 3, everyone is chasing 2-0 Sarasota (PP Smashers), who won five of six individual courts they contested and lost just 37 games, the lowest amount of any team in the division. A trio of squads sits at 1-1 in the flight – Orange/Seminole (Sanlando), Duval (Williams Y) and IRMSL (Buttonwood).

Play picks back up on Sunday beginning at 8:00 a.m. For complete flight standings and schedules, click here.

Established in 1980, USTA League has grown from 13,000 players in a few parts of the country in its first year to more than 900,000 players across the nation today, making it the world’s largest recreational tennis league.

USTA League was established to provide adult recreational players throughout the country with the opportunity to compete against players of similar ability levels. Players participate on teams in a league format, which is administered by the USTA through its 17 sections. The league groups players by using six National Tennis Rating Program (NTRP) levels, ranging from 2.5 (entry) to 5.0 (advanced). USTA League is open to any USTA member 18 years of age or older.
