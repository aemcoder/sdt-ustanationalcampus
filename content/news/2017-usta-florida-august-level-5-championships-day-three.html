USTA Florida August Level 5 Championships

Eight Players Earn Division Titles

August 7, 2017

style
article-hero

Monday marked the conclusion of the 2017 USTA Florida August Level 5 Championships, as eight junior players walked away division champions. Overall, six seeds – including three No. 1 seeds – claimed divisional crowns to go along with a pair of unseeded victors.

One step ahead of the rest of the draws throughout the tournaments, the Boys’ 12s only contested its final on Monday. However, it turned out to be the longest of the eight championship tilts thanks to tremendous efforts from No. 3 seed Yubel Ubri and No. 6 seed Oumar Diallo. Diallo was able to secure the opening set 6-3, but Ubri stormed back in the second six games to two. The match tiebreak was extended all the way to 17-15, with Ubri finally coming out on top.

Top seed Mateo Mendoza earned the title in the Boys’ 14s division, posting a 6-0, 6-2 result in the final over unseeded Samuel Frizelle. Frizelle was coming off an impressive performance earlier in the day, upsetting No. 2 seed William Mroz 6-3, 6-2. But it was Mendoza’s determination that carried him through, as he was pushed to the brink in his semifinal encounter against unseeded Yair Gritzman, which ended in Mendoza’s favor by the slightest of margins, 6-1, 4-6, 10-8.

Sixth seed Nicolas Cabrera was a dominant force on Monday en route to the Boys’ 16s crown. He breezed past Colin Tavares 6-2, 6-0 in the semifinals to begin the day. In the final he was up against unseeded John Ziolo, who had survived a 7-6(4), 2-6, 10-4 battle with No. 2 seed Carter Morgan. Cabrera raced out early and never allowed Ziolo to dig into the match, finishing it off 6-1, 6-1.

Just as with the Boys’ 14s, the championship in the Boys’ 18s featured the draws top seed versus an unseeded challenger. No. 1 seed Alex Guzhva had lived up to his seeding coming into the match, while Hunter Robbins narrowly got through his semifinal over No. 7 Victor Stoyanov 6-3, 3-6, 10-8. It would be Guzhva who was able to polish off an impressive three days, taking the match 6-2, 6-1. Guzhva dropped a combined 13 games total in his five matches.

The only division to have its top two seeds represented in the final was the Girls’ 12s. No. 1 seed Lilly Pleasant and second seed Isabella Rojas Rigoris were on cruise control through the first four rounds to reach the championship. Neither had lost a set while Rojas Rigoris had only surrendered seven games heading into the contest. In the end, Pleasant recorded the victory by a 6-3, 6-3 score line to put her at a mere 17 games lost for the event.

In a draw that saw seeds being taken out right from the start, third seed Alyssa Perdomo and No. 4 seed Katie Heller managed to navigate their way to the finals of the Girls’ 14s. Both looked solid in their morning semifinal, coming through in straight sets. That set up a tightly-contest championship, with Perdomo pulling out a pair of tense 6-4 frames for the title. Perdomo worked her way through the draw without the loss of a set.

Victoria-Jacynthe Oppel capped off her stunning run to the championship in the Girls’ 16s division. She had already collected wins over No. 1 seed Mary Lavin and No. 5 seed Samantha Maras in straight sets to reach the final, while eighth-seeded Hope Moulin made her way to the championship without the loss of a set. The contest would go the distance after Oppel toughed out the opening set in a tiebreak seven points to three and Moulin grabbed the second frame 6-4. The match tiebreak was a tight one, which went the way of Oppel by the slim margin of 10-7.

The Girls’ 18s division was the only one to be represented by a matchup of unseeded players. Both finalists upset a seed in the semifinals, with Abigail Rencheli defeating No. 6 Isabella Montana and Danna Taylor taking out fifth seed Nikki Yanez. The final score line was one fitting for a contest of evenly matched players, with Rencheli able to secure the title by virtue of a 3-6, 7-5, 10-4 triumph.
