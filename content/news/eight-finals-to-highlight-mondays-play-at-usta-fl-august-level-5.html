Eight Finals to Highlight Monday's Play

August 5, 2018

Sunday trimmed the eight divisions down to the finals on Sunday at the 2018 USTA Florida August Level 5 Championships. The day began with quarterfinal action and ended with just two players in each draw left standing with their eyes on the trophy.

Among the finalists are six top seeds along with six players seeded second. In the boys’ 12s and boys’ 18s, the championship match will feature the No. 1 and No. 2 seed; this is also the case for all girls’ divisions except the 12s.

The eight divisional finals matchups are listed below.

Boys’ 12s: No. 1 Nicholas Reeves (Boca Raton) vs. No. 2 Asror Ismoilov (Delray Beach)

Boys’ 14s: Daniel Berenblit (Palm Coast) vs. No. 2 Dylan Chang (Miami)

Boys’ 16s: Andrei Leonov (Tampa) vs. No. 3 William Mroz (Bradenton)

Boys’ 18s: No. 1 David Burns (Fleming Island) vs. No. 2 Nicholas Cary (Lutz)

Girls’ 12s: No. 1 Sam Grosjean (Boca Raton) vs. Alex Ackman (Winter Garden)

Girls’ 14s: No. 1 Chelsea Bluestein (Naples) vs. No. 2 Isabella Rojas Rigoris (Doral)

Girls’ 16s: No. 1 Ava Givone (Delray Beach) vs. No. 2 Anna Babayan (Bradenton)

Girls’ 18s: No. 1 Stephanie Sanchez (Hollywood) vs. No. 2 Nyjah Head (Orlando)

Reeves was forced to rally in the semifinals versus third-seeded Enrique de la Plaza (Hallandale Beach), which he did so successfully for the 3-6, 6-3, 10-6 victory. After surviving a match tiebreak in the opening round, Ismoilov has been on cruise control, dropping a mere eight games in his three other matches.

Berenblit’s path to the final could not have been much tougher. After he stunned No. 1 seed Facundo Olmedo (Doral) on Saturday, he backed that up with a 7-6(8), 6-4 defeat of No. 8 seed Peyton Williams (Weston) in the quarterfinals and a tough 6-3, 7-5 triumph over No. 6 Pablo Paternostro (Weston) in the semifinals. Meanwhile, Chang has yet to be challenged, winning four matches in straight sets and being pushed to 6-4 just once.

Leonov and Mroz both came back from the brink of elimination to lock up a spot in the final. The scare for Leonov took place in the quarterfinals, where he saved match points to oust another unseeded competitor Daniel Green (Boca Raton) 5-7, 6-1, 11-9. He then got past No. 6 Brett Blair (Sarasota) 7-6(4), 6-1. Mroz saw himself extend to a match tiebreak twice on the day. Both times he claimed the opening set but could not close things out easily, which nearly cost him in the quarterfinals against Alexey Timokhine (Orlando), where he also saved match points to prevail 6-4, 2-6, 14-12. Mroz also bounced back from being swept in the second set by No. 8 Ty Rothchild (Tampa) in their semifinal encounter to win 6-2, 0-6, 10-2.

Burns battled past unseeded Christopher Mendivil (Jupiter) 6-3, 7-5 in the quarterfinals, by far his toughest match for the tournament. Cary also had a relatively easy day on court, as he has continued to look sharp following his opening round contest, where he produced a successful comeback effort to defeat Andy Mackler (Lake Mary) 4-6, 6-4, 10-5.

Neither Grosjean nor Ackman have surrendered a set so far. Grosjean has lost just eight games in her four matches while Ackman has posted four 6-0 sets, including one in her 6-0, 6-4 upset of sixth seed Aleksandriya Dzheneva (Melbourne) in the semifinals.

After making quick work of their matchups on Saturday, both Bluestein and Rojas Rigoris were up against more formidable challenges on Sunday. Bluestein has still not lost a set, but she needed to dig deep to take down No. 5 seed Francesca Salvato (Miami Beach) 7-5, 7-5 in the quarterfinals. Rojas Rigoris had her back against the wall in the same round, trailing by a set to No. 6 Jessica Maras (Lake Mary) before mounting a rally for a 4-6, 6-2, 10-3 triumph. The two finalists were much more efficient in the semifinals, losing a combined nine games.

Givone and Babayan have lived up to their top billing in moving through the draw to the finals. No. 1 Givone has not let a set get closer than 6-4 while the most games she has lost in a match is six. Babayan has gotten stronger with each round, powering her way into Monday on the strength of a 6-2, 6-1 defeat of unseeded Ayami Maehama (North Miami Beach) in the semifinals.

Perhaps the two most dominant players in the event have been Sanchez and Head. Sanchez has dropped two games, which even with receiving a retirement in the quarterfinals is still a stunning total. She will be riding high on Monday following her 6-0, 6-0 sweep of Samantha Maras (Lake Mary) to finish off Sunday. Not only has Head posted four consecutive straight-set wins in this tournament, but she has now built up a winning streak of 10 matches on the courts of the National Campus after earning the girls’ 16s title at the 101st American Tennis Association National Championships on Friday.
