2018 USTA FL 55 & Over 3.0/4.0 Sectional Championships

Teams Battle Soggy Conditions, Stiff Competition on Sunday

May 6, 2018

style
article-hero

A rainy morning resulted in a late night on day two of the 2018 USTA Florida Adult 55 & Over 3.0/4.0 Sectional Championships, hosted by the USTA National Campus. However, as the sun went down, Sunday’s entire schedule of play still managed to be completed.

After a full two days of action, all that remains are the three divisional championship matches.

The women’s 3.0 final will feature Leon taking on Duval (Southside Tennis Center). Leon earned the right to represent Flight 1 in the championship dual after finishing in the top spot over Alachua (300) and IRMSL (Magnolia Lakes) via the tiebreaker procedure. All three squads finished with 3-1 overall records, but Leon collected more court wins (eight) than Alachua and IRMSL (seven). Duval made it much more straightforward in Flight 2, posting a perfect 4-0 team record behind the strength of nine individual matchup victories.

After winning semifinal encounters on Sunday evening, Pinellas (SPTC) and Collier (Pelican Bay North) will go head-to-head for the women’s 4.0 title. Pinellas secured a semifinal berth by virtue of sweeping the competition in Flight 2, while Collier did the same in Flight 4. In the semifinal round, Pinellas slipped by Sarasota (BNR) after winning at lines one and three. The clinching victory was delivered at the top position, as Pinellas prevailed 2-6, 6-3, 1-0. Collier was even closer to elimination in their semifinal contest with Sarasota (LWR), losing the first set on all three courts. They were unable to rally at line three, but managed to do so at the top two spots; Collier claimed line one 2-6, 6-4, 1-0 and line two 2-6, 6-0, 1-0.

Flight 3 proved to be far and away the toughest in the men’s 4.0 competition, as its top two finishers have advanced to Monday’s divisional final. Hillsborough (River Hills) won the flight despite tying atop the standings with Collier (The Strand) and Volusia (Ormond Tennis Center). Hillsborough posted the most individual court wins, moving them safely into the semifinals. With the division being composed of just three flights, one second-place team would advance to the semifinals via a wild card; that distinction went to Collier, thanks to losing fewer sets (eight) than all of the other flight runners-up.

Both teams will bring a wealth of confidence into the final. Hillsborough was impressive in beating Broward (Parkland), a team that won all nine courts contested and surrendered only one set in Flight 1. Meanwhile, Collier swept Flight 2 representative Duval (Eagle Harbor). In their meeting as part of round robin action in Flight 3, Collier defeated Hillsborough by a 2-1 margin.

The trio of divisional finals is set to begin at 8:30 a.m. on Monday. A full listing of the 3.0 results can be found here, the 4.0 women scores here, and the 4.0 men results here.

Established in 1980, USTA League has grown from 13,000 players in a few parts of the country in its first year to more than 900,000 players across the nation today, making it the world’s largest recreational tennis league.

USTA League was established to provide adult recreational players throughout the country with the opportunity to compete against players of similar ability levels. Players participate on teams in a league format, which is administered by the USTA through its 17 sections. The league groups players by using six National Tennis Rating Program (NTRP) levels, ranging from 2.5 (entry) to 5.0 (advanced). USTA League is open to any USTA member 18 years of age or older.
