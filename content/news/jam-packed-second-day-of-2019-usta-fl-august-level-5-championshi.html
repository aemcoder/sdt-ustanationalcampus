Jam-Packed Schedule Sunday

August 4, 2019

style
article-hero

With only one round being played in seven of the eight divisions on the opening day of the 2019 USTA Florida August Level 5 Championships, Sunday’s schedule was packed with match play from sun up to sun down.

Highlighted amongst the large number of results were three more top seeds falling along with No. 2 Cooper Jackson (Tampa) and seventh-seeded Ari Siegel (Bradenton) advancing to the girls’ 18s final.

Sunday could not have started better for boys’ 12s No. 1 Carlos Silvestre (Doral), as he turned in a nearly flawless 6-0, 6-0 effort to ease into the quarterfinals. There he ran into No. 7 Tadevos Mirijanyan (Palm Coast), where he staked himself a one-set advantage before seeing Mirijanyan rally back for the 4-6, 6-2, 10-3 upset. Also getting knocked out of the main draw was boys’ 18s top seed David Burns (Fleming Island). His contest with Andrei Leonov (Tampa) was eerily similar to his opening round match; however, he could not pull out the tightly-contested match tiebreak, coming up just short 3-6, 7-5, 12-10.

On the girls’ side, No. 1 Lilly Pleasant (Orlando) played a solid match in the morning to get through to the quarterfinals in the 14s draw. That put her across the next from fifth-seeded Nadia Rodewald (Clearwater), who was nearly sent into consolation play on day one. Rodewald breezed through the first set 6-3 before battling her way to the finish line by a slim 7-5 margin to secure her spot in the semifinals.

Continuing to be the one division on schedule, the matchup for the girls’ 18s title was determined on day two of the event. In the top half, Siegel took advantage of the No. 1 seed being upset on Saturday, besting unseeded Jingyi Li (Bradenton) 6-3, 6-3 in the quarterfinals before outlasting No. 6 Chelsea Bluestein (Naples) 6-4, 3-6, 11-9 in a marathon semifinal encounter. Second seed Jackson continued her dominating ways in the bottom half of the draw, dropping a mere nine games in dispatching No. 8 Maya Shanok (Boca Raton) and fourth-seeded Samantha Maras (Lake Mary) on the day.

Perhaps the most impressive player on the weekend has been boys’ 14s top seed Oumar Diallo (Miramar). He began Sunday with his second 6-0, 6-0 result, only to follow that up with a commanding 6-2, 6-1 score line over No. 8 Kyle Piqueras (Wellington). Next up for Diallo will be a semifinal contest with No. 7 Hunter Nussbaum (Tampa).

On the brink of a first-round loss Saturday, No. 1 Adrian Pawlowski (Sarasota) bounced back with a renewed energy in the boys’ 14s draw. He got past a tough unseeded opponent in Peyton Williams (Weston) 6-2, 6-3 and backed that up with a comprehensive 6-2, 6-0 result against Tyler Leaird (Ocala). Pawlowski will need to continue that level, as he takes on No. 7 Santiago Cardenas (Orlando) in the semifinals Monday.

Receiving an opening round bye, and therefore not stepping foot on court at all until Sunday was girls’ 12s No. 1 seed Marcella Roversi (Doral). Perhaps she was a bit slow out of the gates in her first action of the tournament, but she quickly found her rhythm to take care of Sophia Osipova (Ormond Beach) 6-4, 6-0. She then ran into the No. 8 seed Anita Tu (Melbourne) in the quarterfinals, moving on by a final score line of 6-2, 6-4. Roversi is matched up with fourth seed Sopha Cedeno (Lakeland)  in the divisional semifinals.

Another of the top seeds at the top of her game Sunday was girls’ 16s No. 1 Allison Isaacs (Boca Raton). She did not surrender a single game in the round of 16 versus Abigail Radel (Jacksonville), and was also in control from the start in her 6-2, 6-4 defeat of No. 5 Noor Carrington (Port Saint Lucie) in the quarterfinals. Next up for her is sixth seed Lea Wolfe (North Palm Beach).
