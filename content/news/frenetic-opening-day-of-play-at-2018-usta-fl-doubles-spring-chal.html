Frenetic Opening Day of Play

May 26, 2018

style
article-hero

In an effort to complete as many matches as possible Saturday, nearly every duo contested three matches on the day at the USTA Florida Doubles Spring Challenge.

With Subtropical Storm Alberto to hit Central Florida Sunday, the goal of the opening day of action was to finish as many matches outdoors as possible, especially with all divisions other than the boys’ and girls’ 16s being held on the National Campus Har-Tru courts; it is likely the tournament will become an indoor event on day two.

All eight of the main draws were narrowed down to the semifinal round over the course of Saturday. For the majority of divisions, that meant three rounds were completed.

On the boys’ side, the quartet of top seeds safely navigated their way into the final four. However, not all of the No. 1 tandems finished the day undefeated, as Daniel Gale (Boca Raton) and Spencer Gray (Boca Raton) went ahead with their semifinal encounter with third seeds Jericho Grollman (Altamonte Springs) and Logan Zapp (Fleming Island). The matchup would be extended to a decisive tiebreak, but ultimately Grollman and Zapp prevailed 6-4, 5-7, 10-4. They now await the winner of James Bratt (Middleburg) and Gage Sanchez (Jacksonville) versus Finn Anderson (Sarasota) and Charles Putrino (Sarasota) in a battle of unseeded teams.

Tanner Povey (Indian Rocks Beach) and Tristan Stringer (Orlando) received a bye in the first round of the 12s draw, and then followed that up by dropping just five games in their next two matches en route to the semifinals. The only minor upset in the division was No. 5 seeds Charlie Spencer (Longwood) and Finn Willwerth (Jupiter) slipping past the third-seeded duo of Zach Friedland (Hollywood) and Yubel Ubri (Miami Beach) 6-1, 5-7, 10-8 in the quarterfinals.

Pairs seeded one through four make up the boys’ 14s semifinals. No. 1 Martin Mesia (Weston) and Zamaan Moledina (Delray Beach) along with fourth seeds Santiago Cardenas (Tampa) and Andreas Escalona (Weston) have dominated the competition, surrendering a combined six games. In the bottom half of the draw, the second-seeded tandem of Phillip Dell (Bradenton) and Zak Vince (Clermont) plus No. 3 seeds Yair Gritzman (Boca Raton) and Roger Wang (Saint Petersburg) were pushed to the limit in the quarterfinals, surviving match tiebreaks.

In the boys’ 16s division, all that remain are the top four seeds as well. First seeds Jack Anthrop (Orlando) and Emilio Van Cotthem (Fort Pierce) look to be unstoppable, as they posted a pair of 6-0, 6-0 results on Saturday. Next to attempt to slow them down will be No. 3 seeds Carter Morgan (Jacksonville) and Dante Russo (Deerfield Beach), while second seeds Andy Mackler (Lake Mary) and Benjamin Zipay (Osprey) will clash with No. 4 seeds Gabriel Diaz (Rockledge) and Colin Tavares (Titusville) in the other semifinal.

Draws held mostly true to form in the girls’ divisions as well, with the top seeds still alive going into Sunday. While No. 1 Regina Alcobe-Garibay (Miami) and Susana Souhrada (Boca Raton) easily moved into the final four of the 12s, Julia Lares (Sunny Isles Beach) and Aida Oviedo (Port Saint Lucie) were able to prevent the draw from being complete chalk by upsetting the third-seeded team of Meadow Garcia (Orlando) and Catherine Goldman (Pembroke Pines) in the second round.

Top seeds Natalie Block (Plantation) and Brooke Dugarte (Miami), seeking their third title on the year together, raced their way to the semifinals in the 14s division, losing only two games in the process. Duos seeded second, third and fourth are also still in the main draw, but each needed to survive at least one match tiebreak along the way.

When 16s No. 1 seeds Kassandra Di Staulo (Weston) and Hope Moulin (Lithia) finally took the court after receiving an opening round bye and second round walkover, it was a struggle. However, they managed to overcome Anna Babayan (Bradenton) and Ari Siegel (Bradenton) in two tiebreaks. Their semifinal opponent will be another unseeded pair, DJ Bennett (Belleview) and Didi Bredberg Canizares (Wesley Chapel), who upset No. 4 seeds Petra Miszczak (Bradenton) and Marissa Pennings (Clearwater). Results were much more straightforward in the bottom half, where second seeds Paige Herremans (Orlando) and Daniela Porges (Plantation) will clash with No. 3 seeds Emma Fernald (Saint Petersburg) and Cooper Jackson (Tampa).

The top four seeds in the girls’ 18s draw all reached the semifinals without the loss of a set. No. 1 seeds Sophia Edwards (Nokomis) and Alana Wolfberg (Orlando) needed to collect just one victory on the day, which they did so 6-0, 6-4. The going should get much tougher on Sunday, where the fourth-seeded duo of Ayshe Can (Weston) and Rachele Rimondini (Boca Raton) await, having lost three games total so far. The matchup of second seeds Casey Accola (Winter Garden) and Jamilah Snells (Miramar) taking on No. 3 seeds Karly Friedland (Hollywood) and Jordana Ossa (Miami) should be the initial test for both of those duos as well.
