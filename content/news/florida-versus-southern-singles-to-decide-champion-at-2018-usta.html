The Florida and Southern Sections dominated the competition once again on Saturday before splitting their eight head-to-head doubles contests at the 2018 USTA Donoff Cup. The title will come down to the 12 singles matchups between the two squads. Defending champion Southern currently holds a three-point advantage in the overall team total.

To start off the second day of action, Florida posted a 10-2 mark versus Midwest in singles play while Southern went 11-1 against Middle States. Florida swept all three courts in both the 55s and 70s, dropping just nine games. They also came out on top in two of the three contests in the 60s and 65s, losing line one to Midwest in both divisions. Meanwhile Southern managed to sweep through all but the 55s, where Middle States prevailed at the first position.

Both Florida and Southern continued their impressive level of play in the afternoon doubles session. Florida took all eight matchups with Midwest, which included giving away a mere two games in the 55s behind three 6-0 sets. The only division in which they lost a set was the 65s, where they needed to pull line one out 10-8 in the match tiebreak. Southern faced much more of a test against Middle States, as five of their eight courts went the distance. In the 55s, Southern won 10-3 at the top spot and 10-1 at line two, while also coming through 10-8 on court one in the 65s and 10-6 at line one in the 70s. Middle States took court two in the 65s divisions by a 10-7 margin in the decisive match tiebreak; Southern earned the remaining three matchups in straight sets.

Saturday’s slate ended with the final round of doubles, as Florida and Southern began their clash for the crown while Middle States and Midwest aimed for third.

In the de facto championship match, Florida swept the 55s, Southern swept the 65s, and the teams produced a split in both the 60s and 70s. The Sunshine State’s Erik Luxembourg and Steven Price won 55s line one by a 6-4, 6-4 margin while Stuart Saiki and Tim Keegan grabbed line two 6-1, 6-4. Southern’s Tom Smith and Gert Van den Heever countered in the 65s, winning on court two 6-4, 6-1; Fred Robinson and Marvin Pearson were awarded the top spot when their Florida opponents retired at 6-2, 4-1. For the two divisions that ended in a split, three of the matchups were decided in straight sets while Florida hung on to take line one in the 60s 6-4, 4-6, 10-7.

Middle States will enter Sunday’s singles round with the lead over Midwest after coming out on top in five of the doubles courts the squads contested.

The Donoff Cup is composed of the following age divisions: 55s, 60s, 65s, and 70s. The four Sections have teams competing in those divisions, facing off in round robin dual matches.

A dual match consists of three singles and two doubles matches per division, each counting for one team point. Singles contests will be the best of three tiebreak sets using regular scoring; doubles matches will be two tiebreak sets using regular scoring with a 10-point match tiebreak as a deciding set.

The winner of a team match is determined by the Section that wins the most individual matches; a team can earn up to 20 points in a single dual. If two or more Sections end the weekend with the same number of individual victories, head-to-head results will determine the overall team champion.
