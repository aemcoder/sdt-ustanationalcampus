Divisional Finals Set for Monday

March 17, 2019

article-hero

Sunday marked the second day of play at the 2019 USTA Florida March Level 4 Championships, hosted by the USTA National Campus. With the conclusion of all matches, the eight divisional championship matchups have been set for Monday.

Each division features a draw of 32 with a feed-in consolation through the round of 16. All matches will be two out of three tiebreak sets with a 10-point match tiebreak to be played in lieu of a full third set.

Overall, six of the eight divisional top seeds played their way into the final. The boys’ 16s, girls’ 12s, and girls’ 14s championship matches consist of the No. 1 seed taking on the No. 2 seed. Hope Moulin (Lithia) is the lone unseeded finalists, as she has her eyes set on taking home the girls’ 16s title.

For the boys’ 12s final, No. 1 Quentin Gabler (Miramar) is set to clash with No. 4 Calvin Baierl (Naples). Gabler was locked in an extended battle with sixth-seeded Rama Subodh Pottipati (Boca Raton) in the quarterfinal round, eventually prevailing 6-1, 6-7(4), 10-6 before posting an impressive 6-0, 6-1 victory in the semifinals. Meanwhile, Baierl has not dropped a set so far, losing a mere 11 games in his two contests with seeded competitors on Sunday.

Seeded third in the boys’ 14s competition, Orlando representative Tristan Stringer toughed out a tiebreak in the opening set in his 7-6(4), 6-0 semifinal win over No. 1 seed Martin Mesia (Weston); Mesia was nearly upset by unseeded Kaj Quirjins (Naples) in the previous round. Across the net from Stringer in the final will be No. 2 seed Justin Lyons (Pensacola), who had eased his way into the final four but was pushed to the limit by Ezequiel de la Plaza (Hallandale Beach). Lyons took the first set in a tiebreak, seven points to three, but failed to close it out in straight sets; in the decisive match tiebreak, Lyons came through by a 10-5 margin.

No. 1 Yannik Rahman (Miami) is through to the boys’ 16s final, but nothing was easy for the top seed on Sunday. Unseeded hopeful Zak Vince (Clermont) rallied back in the quarterfinals to push Rahman to a match tiebreak, where Rahman edged out the victory, 10 points to eight. Fourth seed Julen Morgan (Miami) had Rahman on his heels at the start of their semifinal encounter, claiming the first set easily 6-1. A tight second frame went the way of Rahman, 6-4, and he kept the momentum on his side in the crucial match tiebreak, where he won 10-5. The path to the final for No. 2 Santiago Giamichelle (North Miami Beach) has been far less challenging, as he has given away just 13 games over four matches.

Boys’ 18s top seed Anuj Watane (Orlando) has perhaps been the most dominant player of the tournament to date. Losing a total of nine games, he has not allowed an opponent to get closer than 6-3 in any set. Looking to knock him off will be foruth-seeded Jonah Braswell (Sarasota), who survived two match tiebreaks on day one and turned in a pair of comprehensive performances on Sunday, including a 6-2, 6-3 effort versus No. 2 Dillon Blake (Miami).

The matchup between top seed Leila Javaheri (Longwood) and No. 2 seed Aida Oviedo (Port Saint Lucie) for the girls’ 12s title has seemed inevitable as the two continued to quickly dispatch the competition. Javaheri has recorded three 6-0 sets along the way while Oviedo has produced four, including a 6-0, 6-0 win in the second round. Both received their toughest test in the semifinals, where Javaheri defeated unseeded Alba Martinez (Weston) 6-3, 6-3 and Oviedo ousted No. 4 Hayley Roberts (Bradenton) 7-5, 6-0.

No. 1 Lexington Reed (Orlando) took full advantage of the top half of the girls’ 14s draw opening up, defeating two unseeded players on day two to cruise into the championship contest. On paper, second-seeded Sophie Llewellyn (Tarpon Springs) had a much more difficult road Monday ahead of her, but she got by No. 8 Blake Rothchild (Tampa) 6-2, 6-3 in the quarterfinals and No. 4 Hudson Herzog (Delray Beach) 6-2, 6-1 in the semifinals.

Third-seeded Nikki Yanez (Sarasota) played the majority of the crucial points well to fight her way past girls’ 16s No. 1 seed Grace Levelston (Vero Beach) 6-3, 4-6, 11-9 in a high-quality battle. Moulin did the same in the previous round when she came up against second seed Anushka Khune (Plantation), putting together a spirited rally to prevail 5-7, 6-2, 10-4. Moulin was able to successfully follow that up by taking a semifinal matchup of unseeded players 6-3, 6-3 over Maria Drobotova (Deerfield Beach).

Playing solid tennis from the opening round, top seed Sofia Rojas (Sarasota) looks primed to claim the girls’ 18s crown. Her opponent will be No. 8 Karly Friedland (Hollywood), who was also on cruise control until the semifinals, where she was locked in a marathon battle with No. 4 Ariel Johnson (Coconut Creek). Johnson raced through the opening frame, 6-2, and put herself in a position to close out the match comfortably. However, Friedland managed to stay in the match, pushing the second set to a tiebreak, where she dominated seven points to one. In a back-and-forth match tiebreak, Friedland edged out Johnson, 13-11.
