Texas Looks for Another Sweep

November 10, 2018

article-hero

The second day of the 2018 USTA Junior Team Tennis 18 & Under National Championships, held at the USTA National Campus, saw action move from group play to the championship playoff portion of the weekend. By the end of the day, the duals for the Intermediate and Advanced titles were set, with Texas having a chance to take both just as they did last Sunday in the 14U Championships.

The four teams moving into the Intermediate semifinals were Mid-Atlantic (Metro Aces), Northern (BLS), Middle States (Delaware Nightmare), and Texas (ATA Knightmares). Northern produced the most comprehensive performance, going 3-0 in their Flight B duals thanks to 12 court victories and 26 sets collected, while Mid-Atlantic finished undefeated in Flight A after winning 12 individual matchups behind 24 sets claimed. Middle States also posted a 3-0 team record, but found much tougher competition in Flight C, ending with just nine court wins and 19 sets going their way. Taking a much different route to the semifinals was Texas, which actually lost head-to-head versus Southern California (Supreme Aces) 49-47 but was absolutely dominant in their other two duals; Texas closed out group play with more games, sets, and individual matchup wins than Southern California to earn the semifinal berth from Flight D.

Northern was able to edge out Mid-Atlantic 42-40 on the strength of a 6-2, 6-0 effort in girls’ doubles. They also fought their way to victories via comebacks in boys’ doubles (3-6, 6-1, 1-0) and girls’ singles (1-6, 6-1, 1-0). Mid-Atlantic stayed close with an easy 6-1, 6-2 win at boys’ singles but needed a more one-sided final margin than the 5-6, 6-3, 1-0 in mixed doubles. There was far less drama in the other semifinal, where Texas took down Middle States 53-30. Even though Middle States came out on top in the girls’ singles and boys’ doubles contests, both of those went to match tiebreaks. Texas won the other three courts in straight sets, with only one set going to a tiebreak.

On the Advanced side, the field was whittled down to the final four of Midwest (RCC), Northern (SLP McCoy), Southern (Angels), and Texas (ATA Dream Team). None of those squads were challenged in group play, as both Midwest and Northern dropped just one of the 15 individual matchups over the course of three duals in Flight A and B, respectively. Texas was just behind with 13 court wins and 27 sets to their credit in Flight D; Southern won 12 individual contests and 25 sets overall in Flight C.

Despite Northern winning the boys’ singles in straight sets, Midwest claimed the other four courts for an impressive 55-43 semifinal win. Both the girls’ singles and boys’ doubles were tight, ending 6-5, 6-3, but Midwest cruised in the girls’ and mixed doubles, losing a mere six games. In the other semifinal, Texas set the tone early, dropping just two games on the singles courts in their 50-27 defeat of Southern. The victory was capped off with a 1-6, 6-1, 1-0 comeback in boys’ doubles, making the deficit for Southern much too deep to overcome, despite the fact they prevailed in girls’ and mixed doubles in match tiebreaks.

All matches are set to get underway at 8 a.m. Both championship duals will be broadcast live and can be watched here.

Established in 1991, USTA Junior Team Tennis provides youth with all of the health and social benefits of tennis in a coed, fun and competitive environment – all while allowing participants to compete with and against individuals of similar ages and skill levels.

With more than 85,000 players competing nationwide, USTA Junior Team Tennis brings players together to enjoy camaraderie and teamwork, providing a fun environment for kids to learn that succeeding is really more about how they play the game – win or lose.
