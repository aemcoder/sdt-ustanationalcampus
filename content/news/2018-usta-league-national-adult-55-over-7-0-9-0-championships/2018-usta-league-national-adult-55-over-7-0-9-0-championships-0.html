2018 USTA League National Adult 55 & Over 7.0/9.0 Championships

League Nationals Semifinals Set

October 27, 2018

style
article-hero

The stage is set for the semifinals of the 2018 USTA League National Adult 55 & Over 7.0/9.0 Championships at the USTA National Campus.

The competition was close, as tiebreak procedure was necessary to determine many of the semifinal seeds.

A trio of teams comes into tomorrow’s 7.0 Women’s semifinals unscathed through the second day of play, where Southern California (Winner’s Tennis Club – Solana Beach Calif.), Eastern (Quest Racquet Club – Mahwah, NJ) and Florida (Countryside Country Club – Clearwater, Fla.) sit with a 4-0 team score. Southern California earns the top seed winning 10 of their 12 individual matchups. The second and third seed required tiebreak procedure to be the decider, as both Eastern and Florida completed day two with nine individual wins, and nine sets lost. Eastern’s 79 game losses, bested Florida’s 99, earning Eastern the second seed and Florida the third. Rounding out the 7.0 Women’s field is Caribbean (Independent – Puerto Rico) who finished with a 3-1 team record, and winning nine individual matches.

After two days of tight competition, all semifinal seeds in the 7.0 Men’s division additionally came down to tiebreak procedure. As for the first and second teams, Caribbean (Independent – Puerto Rico) and Texas (Huffhines Tennis Center – Richardson, Texas) finished Saturday with a team score of 4-0. Caribbean would earn the number one seed with their 9-3 individual record, just one victory more than Texas’ 8-4 finish. The second half of semifinalist spots however, would go all the way to games lost. Both Missouri Valley (Topeka Country Club – Topeka Kan.) and Southern (Highland Ridge – Marietta Ga.) finished with a 3-1 team score, a 9-3 individual score, and nine sets lost. Missouri earned the third seed, as they lost 81 games, compared to Southern’s 85.  

A triad of teams comes into the 9.0 Men’s finals undefeated. A tiebreak was necessary to determine seeds one and two, as Midwest (South Barrington Tennis Club – Barrington, Ill.) and Southern (Sea Pines Country Club – Hilton Head, SC) boast a 4-0 team score, and 10 individual wins. However Midwest’s seven dropped sets bests Southern’s eight, earning Midwest the top seed and Southern a very close second. In the third seed slot is Missouri Valley (Kansas City Racquet Club – Merriam, Kan.) who finished the day with a 4-0 team score and a 9-3 individual score. Florida (Palma Ceia – Tampa, Fla.) emerged as the best one-loss team to round out the field.

The race for the 9.0 Women’s title is an intense one, as all four semifinalist teams come in to tomorrow’s action at 4-0 apiece. To determine the top two seeds, the decision would come down to games lost, as Southern California (Lower Peters Canyon Park – Irvine, Calif.) and Hawaii (Castle High School Tennis – Kaneohe, Hawaii) both completed day two with 10 individual match wins, two individual match losses, and five sets dropped. It would be Southern California’s 80 games lost that would inch them ahead of Hawaii, who lost 81. Northern California (Gold River Racquet Club – Gold River, Calif.) secured the third seed with a 9-3 individual score record. Rounding out the 9.0 division semifinals is Texas (Life Time Athletic – Plano, Texas) who completed Saturday’s action with a 8-4 individual match record.

A full listing of the updated divisional standings can be found at the following links: 7.0 Women; 7.0 Men; 9.0 Women; 9.0 Men

Sunday’s semifinal duals will take to the courts at 7:30 a.m. with the championship and third-place duals to follow.

Established in 1980, USTA League has grown from 13,000 players in a few parts of the country in its first year to more than 900,000 players across the nation today, making it the world’s largest recreational tennis league.

USTA League was established to provide adult recreational players throughout the country with the opportunity to compete against players of similar ability levels. Players participate on teams in a league format, which is administered by the USTA through its 17 sections. The league groups players by using six National Tennis Rating Program (NTRP) levels, ranging from 2.5 (entry) to 5.0 (advanced). USTA League is open to any USTA member 18 years of age or older.
