After some afternoon rain opening day of the 2018 USTA League National Adult 55 & Over 7.0/9.0 Championships has wrapped up at the USTA National Campus. The tournament marks the fourth consecutive weekend of USTA League Nationals action to take place in Orlando.

Listed below are the teams bringing 2-0 records into Saturday’s action.

7.0 Women: Southern California (Winner’s Tennis Club – Solana Beach, Calif), Northern California (Clovis Tennis Club – Clovis, Calif.) , Caribbean (Independent – Puerto Rico), Florida (Countryside Country Club – Clearwater Fla.), Eastern (Quest Racquet Club – Mahwah, NJ), Intermountain (Park City MARC – Park City, Utah)

7.0 Men: Missouri Valley (Topeka Country Club – Topeka, KS), New England (Westfit Racquet & Fitness Club – Westford, MA), Southern (Highland Ridge – Marietta, GA), Texas (Huffhines Tennis Center – Richardson, TX), Intermountain (DTC – Nevada), Caribbean (Independent – Puerto Rico)

9.0 Women: Northern California (Gold River RC), Southern California (Lower Peters Canyon Park – Irvine, Calif.), New England (Waltham Athletic Club – Waltham, Mass.), Southern (Summit Family YMCA – Newnan, Ga.), Hawaii (Castle High School – Kaneohe, Hi.), Texas (Life Time Athletic – Plano, Texas)

9.0 Men: Midwest (South Barrington Tennis Club – Barrington, IL), Northern California (Canon S and TC – California), Mid-Atlantic (L’hirondelle Club of Ruxton – Ruxton, MD), Southern (Sea Pines Country Club, Hilton Head, SC), Southern California (Nellie Gail Ranch Tennis Club – Laguna Hills, Calif.), Missouri Valley (Kansas City Racquet Club- Merriam, KS)

A full listing of the updated divisional standings can be found at the following links: 7.0 Women; 7.0 Men; 9.0 Women; 9.0 Men

Match play continues at 7 a.m. Saturday morning.

Established in 1980, USTA League has grown from 13,000 players in a few parts of the country in its first year to more than 900,000 players across the nation today, making it the world’s largest recreational tennis league.

USTA League was established to provide adult recreational players throughout the country with the opportunity to compete against players of similar ability levels. Players participate on teams in a league format, which is administered by the USTA through its 17 sections. The league groups players by using six National Tennis Rating Program (NTRP) levels, ranging from 2.5 (entry) to 5.0 (advanced). USTA League is open to any USTA member 18 years of age or older.
