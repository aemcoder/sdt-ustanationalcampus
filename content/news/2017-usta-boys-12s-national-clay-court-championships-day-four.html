Day Four

July 20, 2017

It took four days but Thursday became the first to see its full schedule of play completed at the 2017 USTA Boys’ 12s National Clay Court Championships, part of the USTA adidas Junior National Championship Series, held at the USTA National Campus in Orlando, Fla. Both the singles and doubles main draws are at the semifinal stage heading into Friday.

Only one of the top four singles seeds has managed to stay alive in the hunt for a national championship – No. 4 seed Aidan Kim (Milford, Mich.). Kim has done so in commanding fashion, as he has still yet to drop a set in five matches. No. 24 seed Kaylan Bigun (Surprise, Ariz.) delivered Kim his biggest test so far, pushing the first set to a tiebreak, but Kim claimed it seven points to four and rattled off the second set quickly 6-1.

Next up for Kim is No. 16 seed Garen Spicka (Las Vegas, Nev.), who has been at the top of his game the entire week. Spicka won a set by the score of 6-0 in each of his first three rounds before taking out the No. 7 seed in the round of 16 in straight sets. He came through with perhaps his most impressive performance in his quarterfinal matchup with second-seeded Learner Tien (Irvine, Calif.), winning the opening set 6-4 and cruising in the second 6-1.

In the other semifinal, No. 11 seed Raghav Jangbahadur (Palo Alto, Calif.) and unseeded Juncheng Shang (Naples, Fla.) will face off. Jangbahadur clashed with top seed Cooper Williams (New York, N.Y.) in what ended up being the only three setter on the day. Jangbahadur raced through the first set 6-1 but Williams was able to come back to take the second 6-2. The decisive set was by far the tightest, with Jangbahadur emerging victorious six games to four.

Shang has put together an impressive run to reach the semifinals. His Cinderella story has featured triumphs over three seeded players, starting with the No. 17 seed in the second round. He then posted a 6-0, 7-6(4) upset over the No. 3 seed in the round of 32. In his quarterfinal encounter, Shang was absolutely dominant against No. 26 James Rico (Cary, N.C.), winning 6-1, 6-2. All five of his victories have been recorded in straight sets.

As some duos were faced with the formidable task of playing doubles matches on the day, all doubles round of 16 and quarterfinal matchups were played using short sets. That was the case for the fourth-seeded tandem of Thomas Faurel (Scottsdale, Ariz.) and Joseph Phillips (New York, N.Y.), which finished off their second round match early in the morning to kick off the day. They then defeated No. 15 seeds Nicholas Herdoiza (West Bloomfield, Mich.) and Daniel Stojanov (Novi, Mich.) 4-2, 4-1. In the quarterfinals, Faurel and Phillips overcame No. 7 seeds Aaron Sandler (Huntingdon Valley, Pa.) and Alexander Sterin (Richboro, Pa.) 2-4, 5-3, 10-5.

Across the net from Faurel and Phillips in Friday’s semifinal will be top seeds Lucas Brown (Plano, Texas) and Cooper Williams. In the short set format, the pair was pushed to a tiebreak in the round of 16 before finishing off No. 13 seeds Aidan Kim and Lucas Pak (Chantilly, Va.) 5-4(5), 4-0. Things were more straightforward against the unseeded duo of Jacob Smith (Daniel Island, S.C.) and Davis Taylor (Atlanta, Ga.), as Brown and Williams won 5-3, 4-1 to move into the semifinals.

The No. 2 seeds Andrew Salu (Rancho Santa Fe, Calif.) and Learner Tien made quick work of their third round contest before surviving 2-4, 4-1, 10-7 versus the eighth-seeded team of Quang Duong (Manhattan Beach, Calif.) and Jason Michel (Henderson, Va.). Their semifinal opponents will be No. 12 seeds Andrew Delgado (High Point, N.C.) and James Rico (Cary, N.C.). The Tar Heel tandem pulled off not one but two upsets on Thursday, starting with a 4-1, 4-1 victory over No. 6 seeds Jesper Ohlson (Tulsa, Okla.) and Garen Spicka. They then clashed with the third seeds Owen Megargee (Rockville, Md.) and Stefan Regalia (Arlington, Va.), turning in a 5-3, 4-1 result to prevent the semifinals from being all four top seeds.

Friday’s matches will again start at 8:00 a.m. The singles semifinals are slated for 9:00 a.m. with the doubles semifinals scheduled for 1:00 p.m. Live scoring for all semifinals can be found here.
