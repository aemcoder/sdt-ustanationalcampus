Five days down and just two remain at the 2019 USTA National Winter Championships, part of the USTA adidas Junior Championship Series. Action continued at the USTA National Campus on Wednesday to kick off 2020, as all draws began to close in on the final rounds.

No. 3 Michael Zheng (Montville, N.J.), the highest remaining seed in the boys’ 16s singles draw, put forth perhaps his most dominant performance of the tournament in breezing past unseeded Zane Ford (Butler, Pa.) 6-1, 6-0. It was just the latest in a string of dominant effort for Zheng, who has now lost a mere 10 games in four matches with no set closer than 6-2. Meanwhile, sixth seed Ethan Quinn (Fresno, Calif.) was up to the challenge he got from No. 11 Maxwell Sheldon (Arlington Heights, Ill.) in the opening frame of their contest, eventually coming through 7-6(2), 6-2. Zheng will take on unseeded Yannik Rahman (Miami) in the quarterfinals and Quinn will be up against No. 17 Masato Perera (Santa Barbara, Calif.).

In other boys’ 16s singles action, No. 17 Michael Ross (Charleston, S.C.) put an end to Will Mayew’s (Cary, N.C.) run with a straightforward 6-3, 6-1 victory, while his next opponent Vignesh Gogineni (Mason, Ohio) easily took the matchup of unseeded hopefuls with Alexander Karman (Port Washington, N.Y.) 6-2, 6-0. Round out the quarterfinalists are No. 5 Kurt Miller (Los Gatos, Calif.), who once again needed three sets to advance, and 16th seed Eric Li (Skillman, N.J.), who moved on via a retirement by Peter Hatton (Syracuse, N.Y.) with the score standing at 6-2, 2-0.

For the boys’ 18s contests on the day, two concluded in straight sets and two were extended to a decisive third frame. Top seed Logan Zapp (Fleming Island, Fla.) was locked in a back-and-forth first set with No. 16 Samir Banerjee (Basking Ridge, N.J.), but managed to tough it out seven games to five. From there it was all Zapp, as he put things away 7-5, 6-1 to reach the semifinals. There he will be across the net from No. 12 Keshav Chopra (Marietta, Ga.) after his solid 6-4, 6-2 effort versus third seed Welsh Hotard (New Orleans); Chopra has now defeated a seeded competitor in three consecutive matches, doing so without surrendering a set.

The other semifinal will see No. 4 Benjamin Koch (Abbeville, La.) and eighth seed Ben Shelton (Gainesville, Fla.) clash. Both needed to rally back from a deficit after losing the opening set, with Koch staving off No. 13 Gavin Young (Apple Valley, Minn.) 4-6, 7-5, 6-3 and Shelton turning the tables on second-seeded Leighton Allen (Austin, Texas) by winning 11 consecutive games en route to a 1-6, 6-0, 6-2 triumph.

Knocking out the No. 1 seed in the girls’ 16s division was not enough to satisfy Eileen Wang (Buffalo, N.Y.), who has backed up that stunning result with two more wins over seeded players. Her latest took place on Wednesday, a 6-4, 6-2 defeat of No. 11 Anushka Khune (Plantation, Fla.). Wang will look to make it four in a row when she battles No. 17 Gavriella Smith (New Orleans). Also in the top half, 10th seed Tomi Main (Seaside, Calif.) and No. 17 Lucinda Gatsiounis (Studio City, Calif.) got through in straight sets to set up their semifinal contest.

The other half of the girls’ 16s draw saw the four favorites book their place in the quarterfinals. That task was not easily accomplished by No. 4 Kida Ferrari (Fairhope, Ala.) and fifth seed Stephanie Yakoff (Fort Lee, N.J.), who rallied their way to similar 4-6, 6-4, 6-4 results. Meanwhile, second-seeded Katie Codd (Carlsbad, Calif.) won 7-5, 6-4 and No. 8 Ann Guerry (Atlanta) cruised 6-4, 6-0.

In the girls’ 18s quarterfinals, all four matches were claimed in two sets. No. 1 Sarah Hamner (Fort Collins, Colo.) struggled to shake off eighth seed Allie Gretkowski (Mount Pleasant, S.C.) in the opening frame, but just like her top-seeded counterpart in the boys’ 18s, she found a way to claim it 7-5 and used that to propel herself to an easy 6-1 second set. Hamner will be the next competitor to attempt to upend Katherine Hui (San Diego), the lone unseeded semifinalist; Hui got the best of fellow unseeded Californian Muskan Mahajan (San Ramon) 6-4, 6-1 on Wednesday.

For the other semifinal matchup, fifth-seeded Emma Staker (Sand Springs, Okla.) is set to play No. 17 Emma Shelton (Gainesville, Fla.). The two will bring in a high level of confidence after solid efforts on the day; Staker dismissed No. 4 Gianna Pielet (El Paso, Texas) 6-4, 6-2 and Shelton ousted No. 15 Lara Schneider (Mount Pleasant, S.C.) 6-1, 6-4.

The boys’ 16s doubles draw is down to four tandems, as the top two seeds and two unseeded pairs remain. No. 1 Lucas Brown (Plano, Texas) and Aidan Kim (Milford, Mich.) were able to edge out sixth seeds Bjorn Swenson (North Oaks, Minn.) and Zheng 7-6(5), 6-4 to advance to take on Quinn and Gabriele Brancatelli (Westhampton Beach, N.Y.). The second-seeded tandem of Louis Cloud (San Antonio) and Sebastian Gorzny (Fountain Valley, Calif.) also had a tough time getting past No. 8 seeds James Lian (Parsippany, N.J.) and Alexander Visser (Westport, Conn.) in a 6-4, 7-6(1) affair; they will now go up against Michael Ross (Charleston, S.C.) and Davis Taylor (Atlanta), who won the lone match to go to a decisive tiebreak by a 6-7(4), 6-4, 10-2 margin.

In boys’ 18s doubles action, three of the top four pairs moved on to the semifinal stage while unseeded Texans Riley Odell (Plano) and Garrett Skelly (The Woodlands) round out the field. Odell and Skelly toppled the No. 1 seeds on Tuesday, following that up with a 7-5, 6-3 takedown of No. 8 Thomas Paulsell (Seattle) and Frank Thompson (Blacksburg, Va.) on Wednesday. Next up for them is a showdown with third seeds Aryan Chaudhary (Santa Clara, Calif.) and Zapp, who shook off the disappoint of not closing out their quarterfinal in straight sets to survive and advance 6-3, 5-7, 11-9. In the bottom half of the draw, No. 2 seeds Hotard and Koch along with the fourth-seeded team of Chopra and Coy Simon (Charleston, S.C.) collected comeback victories that ended with a 10-6 score line in the match tiebreak.

Natalie Block (Plantation, Fla.) and Aubrey Nisbet (Wilmington, Del.), the girls’ 16s top seeds, won their second straight 6-3, 6-3 affair, this time over No. 5 seeds Kaia Wolfe (Rancho Mission Viejo, Calif.) and Kayleight Yun-Thayer (Alpharetta, Ga.). Additionally, the dangerous unseeded pairing of Codd and Main continued to navigate through the draw without the loss of a set, with a contest versus No. 2 seeds Vivian Miller (Sullivans Island, S.C.) and Sanjana Tallamraju (Munster, Ind.) awaiting them on Thursday.

The seeds nearly held up through the quarterfinals matches in the girls’ 18s doubles draw, as the top three all won while the fourth-seeded team of Brooke Killingsworth (Rogers, Ark.) and Staker fell to Brdiget Stammel (Dallas) and Katja Wiersholm (Kirkland, Wash.) 6-4, 6-3. That unseeded duo will be the next foes for No. 1 seeds Gretkowski and Carson Tanguilig (Alpharetta, Ga.), as they have yet to clash with another seeded team. The other semifinal will feature No. 2 seeds Julie Fliegner (Clarkston, Mich.) and Schneider along with three seeds Elizabeth Fahrmeier (Cincinnati) and Megan Heuser (Lindenhurst, Ill.) after those two pairs defeated unseeded teams in straight sets.
