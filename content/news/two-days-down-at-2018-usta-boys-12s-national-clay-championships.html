Two Days Down

The 2018 USTA Boys’ 12s National Clay Court Championships, part of the USTA adidas Junior Championship Series, now has two full days in the book following the completion of Tuesday’s schedule. The slate of matches included five more singles seeds being handed a loss, although the top contenders all remained in the hunt for the title. There were four surprises in the doubles draw, highlighted by the No. 1 seeds going out.

For the second day in a row, a meager eight games were surrendered by the top four singles players, which included another 6-0, 6-0 sweep by second seed Andrew Salu (Sarasota, Fla.). No. 1 Rudy Quan (Sacramento, Calif.) also won his match by the exact same score (6-1, 6-0) as Monday. Third-seeded Alexander Frusina (Conroe, Texas) cruised 6-2, 6-2 while No. 4 seed Alexander Razeghi (Humble, Texas) moved on 6-3, 6-0.

While all of the top 16 seeds advanced in the opening round, that was not the case on Tuesday. The highest-ranked player to go down was 12th-seeded Maxwell Exsted (Savage, Minn.), who was on the wrong end of a 3-6, 6-2, 7-5 battle versus Roman Sancilio (Henrico, Va.). Maximus Dussault (Leesburg, Va.) made much quicker work of No. 13 Maxim Michaels (Marlboro, N.J.), taking the match 6-2, 6-1 to move into the round of 32. Midlothian, Va., native Cole Henceroth was equally impressive in dismissing 14th-seed Mikkel Zinder (Richboro, Pa.) 6-1, 6-2.

Also falling prey to unseeded hopefuls was No. 23 William Semler (Portland, Ore.) and No. 24 Andrew Spurck (Palatine, Ill.). Semler dropped a straight-set decision to Mikel Anderson (Hillsboro Beach, Fla.) 6-4, 6-1; Spurck could not complete the comeback in his 6-4, 1-6, 6-4 loss to Oumar Diallo (Miramar, Fla.).

Other singles results of note included sixth seed Lucas Coriaty (Long Beach, Calif.) and No. 17 Mitchell Lee (Oakland, Calif.) blanking their respective opponent 6-0, 6-0; No. 9 Thomas Faurel (Alpharetta, Ga.), the only player who reached at least the round of 16 in last year’s Championship to be in the draw this year, getting past USTA Florida Bobby Curtis Singles titlist Nathan Blokhin (Plantation, Fla.) 6-2, 6-0; and No. 15 A. Filer (South Bend, Ind.) rallying for a 2-6, 6-0, 6-0 victory against Chika Nwaozuzu (Beachwood, Ohio).

The most stunning result of the day came in doubles. Zinder’s tough day on the court continued, as he and Piotr Andrzejewski (Chicago), the top-seeded duo in the draw, fell 7-5, 6-2 to the unseeded duo of Rohan Belday (West Orange, N.J.) and Blokhin. Conversely, Semler was able to shake off his disappointing loss in singles, teaming up with Matteo Antonescu (Mountain View, Calif.) to best No. 13 Blake Hilsen (Cumming, Ga.) and Jordan Reznik (Great Neck, N.Y.) 7-6(3), 6-3.

 In other doubles upsets, the 15th seeds Anderson and Pierce Garbett (Coral Gables, Fla.) were beaten by Siddharth Moturi (Monterey, Calif.) and Steve Nguyen (Anaheim, Calif.) 7-6(4), 6-2. No. 16 Sancilio and Benjamin Wang (Potomac, Md.), one of just three teams pushed to a match tiebreak on Monday, suffered a 6-1, 6-4 setback to Sunshine State pair Alexander Di Staulo (Weston) and Roy Horovitz (Key Biscayne).

Action continues Wednesday morning beginning at 8:00 a.m. Singles action will kick off the day with doubles to follow in the afternoon.

Among the previous singles champions are current pros Donald Young (2001), Jack Sock (2005), and Tommy Paul (2009). Former doubles champions include collegiate head coaches at BYU (Brad Pearce – 1978), Ohio State (Ty Tucker – 1982), and UCF (Bryan Koniecko – 1999) along with MaliVai Washington (1981), Denis Kudla (2004), Jack Sock (2004 and 2005), and Tommy Paul (2009).
