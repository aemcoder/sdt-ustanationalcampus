Only Finals Day Remains

July 21, 2018

Due to more heavy rain in the area, day six was another challenging one at the 2018 USTA Boys’ 12s National Clay Court Championships, part of the USTA adidas Junior Championship Series. Matches eventually shifted to the indoor courts of the USTA National Campus in order for Saturday’s slate to be completed. Highlighting the day were the singles and doubles semifinals.

Rudy Quan (Sacramento, Calif.) was the big winner on Saturday, advancing to the championship contest in both draws. He held off fourth-seeded Alexander Razeghi (Humble, Texas) 7-5, 6-2 in singles before teaming up with Maxwell Exsted (Savage, Minn.) to rally past No. 4 seeds Razeghi and Cooper Woestendick (Olathe, Kan.) 1-6, 7-5, 6-1 to reach the doubles final. Quan faces sixth seed Lucas Coriaty (Long Beach, Calif.) for the singles crown; Quan and Exsted will be across the net from No. 8 seeds Andrew Salu (Sarasota, Fla.) and Adam Sun (Glendale, Ariz.) with the doubles title on the line.

First serve for Sunday’s singles final is scheduled for 9:00 a.m. and will be broadcast here. The doubles final will follow and will not begin before noon.

Top seed Quan faced his first true deficit of the tournament versus Razeghi, who broke in the opening game of the match. However, both players would struggle on serve throughout the first set, making it tough for either to fully grab momentum. Razeghi put himself in a winning position late in the frame, breaking Quan for a third time to serve for the set at 5-4, but Quan broke back at love; Quan gave away just four more points in the set en route to taking it 7-5.

Whether the missed opportunities got the best of Razeghi or the strength of Quan’s game simply took over, the second set went the way of the No. 1 seed quickly. Trailing 1-0, Razeghi fought off four break points before a fifth was eventually converted by Quan, propelling him to a 4-0 advantage. Razeghi did make a push to get within 4-2, but Quan steadied himself to hold at love and then break one last time for the victory. The seven games lost by Quan were by far the most he’d given away in a match, having surrendered just 12 in his five previous rounds. Razeghi was also the first seeded competitor Quan faced in the event.

Another impressive performance was turned in by Coriaty on Saturday in the form of his 6-3, 6-1 defeat of unseeded hopeful Zhengqing Ji (La Jolla, Calif.). Ji upset a total of four seeded players to reach the semifinals, but struggled on serve against the aggressive game of Coriaty, holding only once in the match. The two traded breaks twice in the opening frame, with Coriaty earning the crucial one at love for 5-3 before serving it out. The second set began just like the first, a quick break for each, before Coriaty stormed through the last five games of the encounter. Ji tossed in three doubles faults at 1-1 to get broken and then failed to cash in on a pair of break points in the very next game.

Quan and Coriaty last squared off earlier this year in February at a USTA National Level 2 event, where Quan prevailed 6-1, 6-1.

The doubles semifinals began outdoors before being moved inside after the heavy rainfall early in the afternoon. Razeghi and Woestendick seemed in control, sprinting out to a 6-1, 4-1 lead while never dropping serve. The 11th-seeded pair of Quan and Exsted closed the gap to 4-3 before play was suspended; when the match resumed, they broke Woestendick at love and battled to claim the set 7-5. The decisive set provided little drama, as Quan and Exsted gave away a mere 11 points to win it 6-1.

In the other semifinal, a high-quality opening frame was eventually closed out by Salu and Sun in a tiebreak, seven points to five, against No. 2 seeds Dylan Charlap (Palos Verdes Estates, Calif.) and Mitchell Lee (Oakland, Calif.). The first set ended just before action was halted, but that did nothing to cool off Salu and Sun, who dominated from the very first point indoors for a 6-1 second set.

Among the previous singles champions are current pros Donald Young (2001), Jack Sock (2005), and Tommy Paul (2009). Former doubles champions include collegiate head coaches at BYU (Brad Pearce – 1978), Ohio State (Ty Tucker – 1982), and UCF (Bryan Koniecko – 1999) along with MaliVai Washington (1981), Denis Kudla (2004), Jack Sock (2004 and 2005), and Tommy Paul (2009).
