Argentina Women Secure Finals Berth, Chile and Team USA Men to Meet Again on Friday

February 1, 2018

style
article-hero

Day three of action at the 2018 BNP Paribas Wheelchair World Team Cup Americas Qualifications featured another tightly-constested women's match along with the final two round robin duals on the men's side.

In their second meeting in two days, the women's team from Argentina once again came away with a 2-1 victory over Colombia, this time securing their berth into the BNP Paribas Wheelchair World Team Cup Finals in Apeldoorn, Netherlands, later this year. For the men, both Team USA and Chile won to setup a rematch on Friday with a trip to Apeldoorn also on the line.

The Colombian women took an early lead Thursday thanks to the efforts of Johana Martinez, who posted a 6-2, 6-4 result in her matchup at the No. 2 singles position against Antonella Pralong. Florencia Moreno was even more dominant for Argentina at first singles, defeating Angelica Bernal 6-1, 6-1. The doubles could have gone either way, with Pralong and Moreno earning the opening frame 6-2 before holding chances to close the match out in straight sets; however, Bernal and Martinez took the second set in a tiebreak, seven points to three. That left the winner of the dual down to the match tiebreak, where the Argentinian women came through emphatically, 10 points to three.

Needing a win on Thursday, Team USA put together a decisive trio of matches to sweep Ecuador. Chris Herman got the dual started by beating Eduardo Lopez 6-2, 6-1 at the second singles spot, which was followed up by a 6-0, 6-1 result from Casey Ratzlaff in his contest versus Francisco Beltran. Herman and Ratzlaff maintained their impressive level of play in doubles, winning 6-1, 6-1 for the team sweep.

In the other men's match, Chile was looking to finish round robin play undefeated while Colombia was battling to keep hopes of advacing to the BNP Paribas Wheelchair World Team Cup Finals alive. Colombian Eliecer Oquendo once again breezed through his singles match, besting Jaime Sepulveda 6-2, 6-2. Chilean Alexander Cataldo wasted little time in evening the team score, losing only one game in taking out Manuel Sanchez at the top spot.

All the drama in the tie took place in the doubles. The first frame went to a tiebreak, where the Colombian duo of Oquendo and Sanchez did everything right for a seven points to zero triumph. Chile's Cataldo and Francisco Cayulef were on the brink of defeat in the second set, but managed to battle their way into another tiebreak, this time prevailing eight points to six. That set the stage for a tense match tiebreak, where both tandems had their chances before the Chileans ultimately came out on top, 10-8.
