2017 ITF Super-Seniors World Team Championships

Team USA Continues Impressive Play

October 12, 2017

style
article-hero

Another full slate of playoff duals was featured in Thursday’s action at the 2017 ITF Super-Seniors World Team Championships, hosted by the USTA National Campus in Orlando, Fla. Matches in all nine cups were contested in the penultimate playoff day.

Team USA’s impressive results continue to be the story of the week, as the American are still alive for the championship in seven of the nine cups. Australia has also been well represented throughout the team competition, as they will play for four team titles on Friday.

Coming in as the favorite to win the Britannia Cup (Men’s 65), top-seed United States lived up to its billing via a 3-0 result against No. 3 Australia. Team USA has yet to drop a rubber during the competition, sweeping through group play before producing consecutive shutouts in the championship playoff rounds. They will be up against Cinderella story Austria, which stunned No. 2 France to win their group and has reached the final thanks to a pair of three-set victories in singles against four seed Italy in the semifinals.

Similarly, the Jack Crawford Cup (Men’s 70) will also feature the pre-tournament favorite taking on an unseeded hopeful. Spain made quick work of No. 4 Switzerland in their semifinal encounter, dropping just nine games on the two singles courts to secure a spot in the final. Meanwhile, the Aussies have taken a much more arduous path to reach the championship tie after stunning third-seeded France in Group C. Australia upset No. 5 United States 2-1 in the quarterfinals thanks to commanding singles play, but found themselves in a live doubles rubber on Thursday against the second-seeded Dutch. After the duos split the first two sets, it was the Aussies who took control, winning the decider 6-0 to move on.

The championship matchup is set for the Bitsy Grant Cup (Men’s 75), where the No. 1 seed Australia and No. 6 seed Netherlands will square off. Team USA wrapped up play in the cup on Thursday, claiming fifth after getting past German 2-1 in a dual decided by the doubles rubber.

The Americans and Aussies will duke it out for the Gardnar Mulloy Cup (Men’s 80) title on Friday after both squads secured 2-1 victories over Germany in earlier playoff action. No. 1 United States surrendered a mere three games against the second-seeded Germans, while No. 3 seed Australia was pushed on the two singles courts. Germany came away with the doubles point in both contests.

All that’s left to be decided in the Men’s 85 Team Cup is the champion, which will be either top seed Team USA or Canada. Australia earned a third-place finish after a 3-0 sweep of Great Britain and Argentina took fifth with an impressive performance against Sweden.

The Kitty Godfree Cup (Women’s 65) will be another battle of the United States and Australia, the cups top two seeds. After being tested by Brazil in the quarterfinals, the No. 1 Aussies triumphed on all three courts against No. 6 Germany, needing three sets to take the top singles position. The Americans had locked up their championship berth after the conclusion of singles against No. 5 Great Britain, doing so in the most dramatic of fashions via a third set tiebreak at line two.

Team USA has advanced to the final of the Althea Gibson Cup (Women’s 70) thanks to a tremendous 3-0 defeat of No. 4 South Africa in the semifinals. Awaiting the United States in the championship round is second seed Netherlands, who outlasted Australia 2-1. The Aussies swept the Kiwis on Thursday to earn third.

Having already defeated No. 2 Germany in round robin championship playoff action in the Queens Cup (Women’s 75), the top-seeded United States will take on No. 4 Great Britain the heavy favorite to lock up the team crown. Germany swept Great Britain on Thursday and can finish no worse the second.

With one final contest remaining in the Doris Hart Cup (Women’s 80), the possibility exists for all three teams in the championship playoff to ended with a 1-1 dual record and 3-3 record in rubbers. The No. 1 Americans own a 3-0 sweep of New Zealand, who in turn swept No. 3 Canada. The American and Canadians will clash on Friday; a Team USA victory would greatly simplify things, giving them the title outright.

The team portion of the international event will wrap up on Friday, as matches will begin at 8:30 am. Following the completion of all championship matches, the team award ceremony will be held to recognize all medal winners. Featuring 123 teams (72 men’s and 51 women’s) representing 31 nations, the 2017 ITF Super-Seniors World Team Championships are being held at the USTA National Campus in Orlando, Fla., from Oct. 8-13. The international tournament is composed of nine cups for the first time in its history, as the Men’s 85 Team Cup is in its first year of existence.
