Open Draws Underway

The 101st American Tennis Association (ATA) National Championships, hosted by the USTA National Campus, began play on Saturday with the first rounds of the five open draws – men’s singles and doubles, women’s singles and doubles, and mixed doubles.

The men’s open singles draw featured only five matches on the day after No. 3 seed Harrison Horton (Tampa, Fla.) withdrew prior to the start of play, moving Fidel Serrano (Dorado, P.R.) into the next round. Ransom Medina (Houston, Texas) and Bjorn Munroe (West Palm Beach, Fla.) were locked in the lone matchup that went the distance, with Medina prevailing 6-2, 4-6, 10-3; next up for Medina is top seed Jordan Harrell (Lithonia, Ga.). Other winners in the draw included Caleb Strother (Pompano Beach, Fla.), Daniel Thornton (Winter Garden, Fla.), No. 4 seed Aaron Goldsmith (Mattapan, Mass.) and James Jackson (Clarksdale, Miss.).

With just eight players making up the women’s open singles draw, that division is already down to the semifinals after the opening day of action. No. 1 seed Jennifer Myers (Winter Haven, Fla.) cruised to a 6-1, 6-0 victory over Angel Sanders (Memphis, Tenn.) to kick off her quest for the title. She will face Aysha Hayes (Pinole, Calif.) on Sunday, who got past Jessica Fischer (Winter Haven, Fla.) by a 6-4, 6-3 margin. In the bottom half, Isabelle Porter (Jupiter, Fla.) defeated Soizette Simmons (Brooklyn, N.Y.) while Chacadyah Lewis (Lake Worth, Fla.) stunned second-seeded Caprice Thomas (Miramar, Fla.) 6-0, 6-1.

Both open doubles draw will see the three teams entered battle for the crown using the round robin format, with one match each day. On Saturday, Harrell and Jackson breezed by Maddox Bose (Winter Haven, Fla.) and Thornton 6-1, 6-0 on the men’s side. Chacadyah and Rishona Lewis (Lake Worth, Fla.) were even more impressive in their sweep over Gloria Gray (Hephzibah, Ga.) and Teresa Kemp (Grovetown, Ga.).

In the mixed open doubles division, all four tandems advancing surrendered a mere three games. No. 1 seeds Azaria Hayes (Pinole, Calif.) and Darryl Hubbard (Oakland, Calif.) downed Bose and Fischer 6-2, 6-1. Their semifinal opponent will be Donovan Spigner and Shayna Spooner (New York), who bested Sunshine State pair Myers and Thornton by the exact same score. The second-seeded duo of Jazmine Davis (Augusta, Ga.) and Harrell had no trouble taking out Zachary Rivera- Salla (Los Angeles) and Simmons 6-1, 6-2. Rounding out the mixed results was the 6-2, 6-1 win turned in by Munroe and Porter against Audley Cornwall (Jacksonville, Fla.) and Simmons.

Play continues on Sunday at 8:00 a.m., which will include the men’s open singles quarterfinals and women’s open singles semifinals.

The ATA was born on November 30, 1916, when representatives from over a dozen black tennis clubs met in Washington, D.C., with the goal of promoting black tennis in America. The organization held the first ATA National Championships at Baltimore’s Druid Hill Park in August of the following year; the tournament featured three total draws – men’s singles, women’s singles, and men’s doubles.

The ATA, which is the oldest African-American sports organization in the United States, has produced a number of the world’s top players and coaches. Most notably among these are Althea Gibson and Arthur Ashe, the first African-Americans to be ranked No. 1 and to win grand slam titles. Others to grow up training at ATA-affiliated clubs and participating in ATA events include current USTA Chairman of the Board and President Katrina Adams, Zina Garrison, Leslie Allen, Lori McNeil, Chanda Rubin and MaliVai Washington.
