Saturday Cut Short Due to Rain

June 23, 2018

article-hero

Saturday’s full slate of matches was nearly completed, but heavy rains late in the afternoon prevented the final contests on court from finishing.

A handful of 7.0 duals were in progress when play was suspended for the day, leaving three of the four flights in slight disarray. Only Flight 4 saw all of its teams record two results on Saturday, as Broward (Midtown) went 2-0 to put themselves just one victory away from moving on to the semifinals. Standing in their way will be Duval (Fleming Island), the only squad to go 0-2 in the flight, while the winner of Sunday’s Polk (City of Winter Haven) and Sarasota (LWR Ranchers) matchup can steal the semifinal berth with a Broward loss.

In other 7.0 action, Duval (Boone Park) sits atop Flight 1 at 2-0, but both Escambia and SMD (Salvadore Park) were on the court with 1-0 records when matches were halted. Pinellas (Seminole Baseline Ballers) are also 2-0, giving them control of Flight 2 so far; Bay (PCRC) also looks like a contender in the flight after producing a sweep in their lone completed dual on Saturday. Flight 3 appears the most unsettled after day one, with two teams at 1-1 and two others at 1-0. Hillsborough (Hunters Green) and Brevard (TTC) both completed its pair of matches, but neither was able to escape unscathed. Alachua and Leon won their only team matches that finished. All four are well within the hunt to reach the semifinals.

The 9.0 division was not impacted by the weather. Heading into Sunday, each squad has one more round robin matchup to play before the championship dual is contested in the afternoon. IRMSL (Buttonwood) is the lone unbeaten in Flight 1, with a contest against 1-1 Pinellas (Tarpon) all that remains between them and a place in the final. SMD (Coral Pine) is in an even better situation in Flight 2, having already defeated the two teams that sit at 1-1 overall; their lone dual left comes against 0-2 Escambia.

Matches continue at 8:00 a.m. Sunday morning, with the 9.0 championship scheduled for 3:00 p.m. A full listing of the 7.0 divisional flights and schedule can be found here, while more details regarding the 9.0 competition can be found here.

Established in 1980, USTA League has grown from 13,000 players in a few parts of the country in its first year to more than 900,000 players across the nation today, making it the world’s largest recreational tennis league.

USTA League was established to provide adult recreational players throughout the country with the opportunity to compete against players of similar ability levels. Players participate on teams in a league format, which is administered by the USTA through its 17 sections. The league groups players by using six National Tennis Rating Program (NTRP) levels, ranging from 2.5 (entry) to 5.0 (advanced). USTA League is open to any USTA member 18 years of age or older.
